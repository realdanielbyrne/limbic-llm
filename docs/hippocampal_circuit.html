Hippocampal CA Subfield Circuitry

CA3 • CA2 • CA1 Representative Subnetwork with Recurrent Collaterals

CA3 Recurrent Collaterals

CA3 pyramidal cells send axon branches back to other CA3 neurons, forming an autoassociative network. This architecture enables pattern completion: a partial cue reactivates the full stored representation. Each CA3 cell may contact ~4% of all other CA3 cells—an extraordinarily dense recurrent system.

Schaffer Collaterals (CA3→CA1)

The third synapse of the trisynaptic loop. CA3 axons project to CA1 stratum radiatum, where NMDA receptor-dependent LTP encodes new memories. This is the synapse most vulnerable to ethanol blockade during alcohol-induced blackouts.

Mossy Fibers (DG→CA3)

Dentate granule cell axons form giant synaptic boutons (marked as large dots) on CA3 proximal dendrites. Each bouton contains hundreds of release sites, making these among the most powerful synapses in the brain. Note: mossy fibers do not innervate CA2.

CA2 Connections

CA2 receives input from CA3 and projects to CA1, but lacks mossy fiber input. Instead, it receives unique projections from the supramammillary nucleus and vasopressinergic afferents. CA2 is specialized for social memory processing.

Entorhinal Cortex Paths

Perforant path (EC Layer II → DG): main cortical input. Temporoammonic path (EC Layer III → CA1): direct bypass route shown as dashed lines. CA1 compares CA3 predictions with direct EC sensory input for novelty/mismatch detection.

Inhibitory Interneurons

Basket cells (CA3) and O-LM cells (CA1) provide GABAergic inhibition (dashed lines) that regulates excitation-inhibition balance. These interneurons are key targets of benzodiazepines and ethanol's GABAergic potentiation.
