# CA3→CA1, skip paths, CA1 comparator, back-projection and the temporal loop: a review of limbic-llm

## Scope

This review covers one track of limbic-llm (commit 3799b36): the serial CA3→CA1 (Schaffer) path, the EC-III→CA1 temporoammonic and EC-II→CA3 direct skip connections, the CA1 match/mismatch comparator and its novelty signal, the CA1→CA3 "op-amp" negative feedback, and the temporal loop with its clamped damped-sinusoid re-entry kernel and time channel. It reads `limbic_llm/ca1.py`, `ca3.py`, `hippocampal_block.py`, `temporal_loop.py`, `subiculum.py`, `docs/design/temporal-loop.md`, `docs/genesis.md` and the result register (`results/evolve/REGISTER.md`), and compares them with the neuroscience and machine-learning literature through late 2026.

The verdict, in brief. Most individual mechanisms in this track have precedent. CA3-as-attention is [Ramsauer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217) together with [Whittington et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04035). The successor store goes back to [Sompolinsky & Kanter 1986](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.57.2861) and its modern dense-memory version [Chaudhry et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04532). The CA1 fusion vector is the heuristic-matching feature of natural-language inference ([Mou et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08422); [Conneau et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02364)). The comparator-as-prediction-error idea is the temporal prediction hypothesis of [Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.05.024). The closed loop that writes a model's own lagged states back into memory is anticipated by the Feedback Transformer [Fan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09402). The time channel is the Laplace stage of [Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00212) and has been put into deep networks before ([Jacques et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04646)). Four things are new or at least unusual: explicit loop-gain analysis of a memory re-injection side channel; the delayed, rectified, per-circuit-damped re-entry kernel; the asymmetric sparse-write/dense-read addressing taken from [Treves & Rolls 1992](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450020209); and a clean small-scale demonstration (T6, T7) that subtractive feedback obeys the closed-loop gain law. The track's main weaknesses are in the biological mapping (a CA1→CA3 pyramidal back-projection that the docs deny exists, a theta analogy that does not match theta) and in two code-level issues: the comparator is mistimed by one step, and the "high-pass" description of the feedback ignores Bode's sensitivity constraint.

## Closest prior work

### Two routes into CA1

The anatomy the repo builds on is standard: EC layer II drives DG and CA3, EC layer III drives CA1 directly on the distal tuft in stratum lacunosum-moleculare, and CA3 drives CA1 through the Schaffer collaterals on proximal apical dendrites ([Amaral & Witter 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0306-4522%2889%2990424-7)). The repo's `HippocampalBlock` reproduces that split. Two refinements matter. First, the EC-II/EC-III split is not clean: EC-II "island" cells project to CA1 and are required for temporal association memory, while "ocean" stellate cells drive DG/CA3 ([Kitamura et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1244634)). Second, CA2 is not a station on the Schaffer path. It receives strong EC-II input and forms a disynaptic EC→CA2→CA1 loop that runs parallel to the trisynaptic one ([Chevaleyre & Siegelbaum 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.04.013)).

The lesion and silencing literature gives the two routes different jobs, and this division is the most useful constraint on the ML design. CA1 place fields and place recognition survive removal of CA3 input ([Brun et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1071089)), so the monosynaptic route alone carries a usable spatial code. Blocking CA3 output transgenically spares incremental learning but impairs rapid, one-trial learning and pattern completion from partial cues ([Nakashiba et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1151120)). The EC-III→CA1 input is needed for temporal association memory ([Suh et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1210125)) and for consolidation ([Remondes & Schuman 2004](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02965)). In a network model, [Schapiro et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0049) made the split computational: the monosynaptic pathway, with overlapping representations, learns statistical regularities, and the sparse trisynaptic pathway stores individual episodes. A 2026 preprint adds that silencing the direct entorhinal input leaves CA1 maps in a familiar environment intact but blocks remapping in a novel one, producing a hybrid map ([Chadney et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.02.736055), preprint). The temporoammonic input is therefore specifically involved in novelty-driven plasticity. limbic-llm's design is consistent with all of this, but it does not yet test the dissociation (Proposed experiments, E2).

### CA1 as comparator, error unit, or instructive target

The comparator view of CA1 has three strands, and they disagree in ways that matter for the code. The classical strand treats CA1 as computing a match/mismatch between what CA3 recalls and what EC reports ([Vinogradova 2001](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.1073); [Lisman & Otmakhova 2001](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.1071); [Lisman & Grace 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.05.002)). Human high-resolution fMRI supports it: CA1 responds to associative mismatch ([Kumaran & Maguire 2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1677-07.2007); [Chen et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.2135211)), and [Duncan et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20933) found CA1 activity graded by how many features of a learned configuration changed. The computational strand makes the mismatch a learning signal. [Ketz et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003067) used theta phase to alternate CA1 between EC-driven and CA3-driven states and trained on the difference. [Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.05.024) formalised the temporal prediction hypothesis, in which CA3 predicts the next EC input and CA1 computes the prediction error, and the PhiNet family imported that circuit into self-supervised learning ([Ishikawa et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14650)). [Barron et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2020.101821) gives the predictive-coding framing.

The cellular strand complicates the "mismatch" reading. Coincident distal (EC-III) and proximal (CA3) input produces dendritic plateau potentials and strong plasticity ([Takahashi & Magee 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.03.007); [Dudman et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2007.10.020)). Conjunctive input of this kind sets CA1 feature selectivity ([Bittner et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4062)) through behavioural-timescale plasticity ([Bittner et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan3846)), and EC-III input directs learning-related changes in CA1 representations ([Grienberger & Magee 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05378-6)). In ML terms, EC-III acts more like a *teacher* or target signal that gates plasticity in CA1 than like a skip connection summed into the output. `ca1.py` uses the TA input both ways, as a fused feature and as one side of a novelty comparison, and the docstring's sentence that "coincidence drives plateau potentials; mismatch signals novelty" combines two different signals: plateaus mark *conjunction*, not mismatch.

On the ML side, the fusion vector φ = [a; r; a⊙r; |a−r|] is exactly the "heuristic matching" feature introduced for sentence-pair inference by [Mou et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08422) and popularised by [Conneau et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02364). The novelty head is trained, through `novelty_alignment_loss`, to regress the z-scored token loss, which makes it a distilled surprise predictor in the sense of Titans ([Behrouz et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663)) and EM-LLM ([Fountas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09450)). It is not an intrinsic comparator readout. That is a reasonable engineering choice, but the comparator structure is not what makes the signal mean "novelty"; the supervision is.

### Skip, dense and cross-layer connections

The EC-III→CA1 path is a long skip from the input stage to a late stage, and the ML analogues are well developed. Gated and identity skips ([Srivastava et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00387); [He et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr.2016.90)) and dense concatenation of all earlier stages ([Huang et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr.2017.243)) are the originals. For transformers, DenseFormer adds learned depth-weighted averages of all earlier block outputs ([Pagliardini et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)). Hyper-connections learn the strength of connections across depths ([Zhu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606)). mHC constrains the hyper-connection mixing to a manifold to restore the identity mapping and keep training stable ([Xie et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880), preprint). MUDDFormer makes dense connections input-dependent and separate for Q, K, V and the residual ([Xiao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12170)). The closest analogue to "re-inject the entorhinal representation deep in the circuit" is value residual learning, where every layer's values are mixed with the first layer's values ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17897)). Cross-layer KV sharing ([Brandon et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12981)) and early-exit or skip-middle designs ([Elhoushi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16710); [Lawson & Aitchison 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21103), preprint) are related but run the other way: they remove depth rather than add a second route. [Lawson & Aitchison 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21103) report that skipping middle layers did not improve the loss-versus-FLOPs trade-off at their scales. [Burns et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15113) (preprint) explicitly connected an attention residual stream between heads to the CA3→CA1 and CA2 skip question. No published transformer I found uses a dedicated comparator between a memory route and a long input skip as limbic-llm does. The pieces, though (long skip, gated mixing, matching features), are all standard.

### CA3: associative and sequence memory

That CA3 retrieval is a modern-Hopfield update and that this update is attention is [Ramsauer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217), building on [Krotov & Hopfield 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01164) and [Krotov & Hopfield 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06996). [Whittington et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04035) went further and showed that a transformer with recurrent positional encodings is the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine ([Whittington et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024)), with keys from MEC-like position codes and values from LEC-like sensory codes. Energy-based transformer blocks make the attractor dynamics explicit ([Hoover et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07253)). The repo's `CA3Autoassociative` (cosine-normalised attention with a learned inverse temperature, iterated with tied weights) is a direct re-implementation of this line.

The successor store (`sequential=True`, keys h_t, values h_{t+1}) is temporally asymmetric Hebbian heteroassociation. It goes back to [Sompolinsky & Kanter 1986](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.57.2861), was proposed for CA3 by [Levy 1996](https://doi.org/10.1002/%28sici%291098-1063%281996%296:6<579::aid-hipo3>3.0.co;2-c) and [Jensen & Lisman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.3.2-3.279), and has a dense-associative-memory form with much higher sequence capacity in [Chaudhry et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04532). [Tang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11982) showed that a temporal-predictive-coding sequence memory is an asymmetric Hopfield network with implicit whitening, and [Salvatori et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08063) built an associative memory trained by predictive coding. [Chaudhry et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04532) also extended their model to sequences with variable timing between states, which bears on the time channel below. The successor representation ([Stachenfeld et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650)) is the multi-step, expectation-valued version of the same idea. The novelty-gated, capacity-limited write (top-k most novel tokens replace the lowest-salience slots, salience decays, retrieval reconsolidates) resembles least-recently-used slot replacement in memory-augmented networks ([Santoro et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06065); [Graves et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5401)) and the kNN memory of [Wu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08913). Its gating signal is the surprise criterion of Titans and EM-LLM. Delta-rule and test-time-regression views of these memories ([Schlag et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174); [Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464); [Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352); [Behrouz et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13173)) are covered in the GDN track and are not repeated here.

### Theta sequences, phase precession, and phase-separated encoding and retrieval

Place cells fire at progressively earlier theta phases as an animal crosses a field ([O'Keefe & Recce 1993](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450030307)). At the population level this compresses behavioural sequences into single theta cycles ([Skaggs et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1002/%28sici%291098-1063%281996%296:2<149::aid-hipo6>3.0.co;2-k)), which appear as forward-sweeping theta sequences ([Dragoi & Buzsáki 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.02.023); [Foster & Wilson 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20345)). These sequences can alternate cycle by cycle between possible futures ([Kay et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.014)). [Jensen & Lisman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.3.2-3.279) explained phase precession as CA3 sequence read-out, and [Hasselmo et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976602317318965) proposed that encoding (EC-dominated) and retrieval (CA3-dominated) occupy opposite phases of a *global* theta cycle; [Buzsáki 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0896-6273%2802%2900586-x) reviews the oscillation itself. These findings bear directly on the repo's oscillatory kernel, and they do not support it in the form claimed (Biological accuracy, point 4).

### Back-projections and feedback inhibition

[Sik et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8085161) described CA1 GABAergic neurons whose axons return to CA3 and the hilus, and [Jinno et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1847-07.2007) characterised such long-range back-projection and double-projection interneurons. Viral tracing has since found *excitatory* non-canonical input to dorsal CA3 from ventral CA1 and the subicular complex, and genetic inactivation of the vCA1→dCA3 projection impaired object-related spatial learning ([Lin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001127)). The same group then showed that CA1 and subicular inputs also reach CA3 inhibitory neurons ([Lin et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.25510)). The back-projection is therefore mixed in sign and organised along the septotemporal axis. It is not a clean inverting amplifier.

For loop stability, the closest neural precedents are inhibition-stabilised networks ([Tsodyks et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.17-11-04382.1997)), amplification by recurrent excitation under inhibitory control ([Douglas et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7638624)), and oscillations created by *delayed* inhibitory feedback ([Brunel & Hakim 1999](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976699300016179)). The last is the same mechanism as the repo's ringing above K≈1.68. The engineering originals are [Black 1934](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1934.tb00652.x) for the closed-loop gain law and [Nyquist 1932](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1932.tb02344.x) for delay-induced instability. Subtracting a lagged estimate of a signal to transmit only what is new is predictive coding in its original retinal form ([Srinivasan et al. 1982](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1982.0085); [Rao & Ballard 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580)), and [Lotter et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08104) used it in deep networks. In LLM steering, a PID-style feedback controller on activations has been published ([Nguyen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04309)).

### Re-entrant loops, contrast, and time in sequence models

Writing a model's own higher-layer states back into a memory that later timesteps read is the Feedback Transformer ([Fan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09402)). Block-recurrent transformers ([Hutchins et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07852)) and TransformerFAM ([Hwang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09173), preprint) are variants, and looped-depth models ([Dehghani et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819); [Zhu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)) recur over depth rather than time. The repo's "episode minus prior" contrast Δ = F − mμ is attention over mean-centred values, a common-mode cancellation that is related in spirit to the Differential Transformer ([Ye et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05258)) and to layer-contrastive and model-contrastive decoding ([Chuang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03883); [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15097)). The connection to activation steering ([Turner et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248)) is drawn in the repo's own notes.

The oscillatory kernel's ML relatives are damped complex-exponential recurrences: RetNet's multi-scale decays, with per-head decay rates spaced geometrically ([Sun et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08621)); the LRU ([Orvieto et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349)); HiPPO and S4 ([Gu et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07669); [Gu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)); and Mega's multi-dimensional EMA ([Ma et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10655)). The time channel's precedents are tighter still. [Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00212) and [Howard et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.5808-12.2014) proposed a bank of leaky integrators with log-spaced rates, which is a Laplace transform of the past, followed by an approximate inverse that yields time-cell-like units. DeepSITH and SITHCon put that construction into deep networks ([Jacques et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04646); [Jacques et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04616)). [Voelker & Eliasmith 2018](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01046) derived time cells from optimal delay networks, the precursor of the Legendre Memory Unit. [Mahto et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12727) imposed a distribution of LSTM timescales chosen to produce power-law memory decay in language models. Contextual position encoding counts tokens through learned sigmoid gates ([Golovneva et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18719)), which is the same "learned salience u_t drives a counter" idea without the leak. The Forgetting Transformer ([Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02130)) puts a learned, data-dependent decay into softmax attention. Biologically, exponentially decaying and ramping "temporal context" cells sit in lateral entorhinal cortex ([Tsao et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0459-6); [Bright et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917197117)), while hippocampal time cells ([MacDonald et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.07.012)) are sequentially tuned and logarithmically compressed ([Cao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.75353)). [Muller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2024.08.006) review transformers and cortical travelling waves as mechanisms for carrying context across time.

## Novelty and anticipation

### Anticipated or re-described

The CA3 module and its successor store are re-descriptions of published work, and the repo's docstrings cite most of it, so this is about framing claims rather than missing credit. The novelty-gated slot write is a recombination of LRU-style slot replacement ([Santoro et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06065)) with a surprise gate ([Behrouz et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663); [Fountas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09450)).

The CA1 comparator is anticipated in form and in concept. Its feature vector is [Mou et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08422)/[Conneau et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02364), and its role as a CA3-prediction versus EC-input error is [Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.05.024) (with [Ketz et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003067) and [Lisman & Otmakhova 2001](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.1071) earlier). It should cite all three.

The EC-III→CA1 skip is a long skip. Without a comparison against value residuals ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17897)) or DenseFormer-style mixing ([Pagliardini et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)), nothing shows that the "temporoammonic" framing adds anything over these.

The closed temporal loop, writing post-injection states back into the store, is a Feedback-Transformer-type design ([Fan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09402)). The genesis claim that "the closed, regulated, timed loop is a new organisation" holds only for the *regulated* and *timed* parts.

The time channel is a fixed-pole diagonal linear system driven by a learned scalar gate. It equals the Laplace layer of [Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00212), uses geometric timescale spacing as in RetNet's multi-scale decays ([Sun et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08621)), and is close to gated counting ([Golovneva et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18719)) with a leak. The design note's statement that nothing tells transformers how much time has passed is too strong: decays in RetNet, the Forgetting Transformer and CoPE all supply such a signal implicitly. What is new is its use as an explicit, separate side channel into a frozen model, together with the what-when evaluation (T1).

### Partially novel

1. *Loop-gain analysis of a memory side channel.* The gain law and the delay limit are textbook ([Black 1934](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1934.tb00652.x); [Nyquist 1932](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1932.tb02344.x)), and delayed inhibitory feedback is known to oscillate ([Brunel & Hakim 1999](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976699300016179)). I found no prior LLM or memory-augmented-transformer paper that writes the characteristic polynomial of a re-injection loop, predicts a critical gain, and confirms it (T6, T7). The PID steering controller of [Nguyen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04309) is the nearest, but it regulates a steering error, not a memory echo. This is worth publishing as a short analysis. The numbers reproduce: solving z^τ − ρz^{τ−1} + K(1−ρ) = 0 at τ = 16, ρ = 0.9 gives K_c = 1.680 and a ringing period of 44.1 tokens.
2. *The re-entry kernel w(Δ) = Aρ^{Δ/τ−1}[cos(2πΔ/τ)]₊ with Δ ≥ τ.* Damped oscillatory kernels are common ([Sun et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08621); [Orvieto et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349)). A rectified, delay-onset kernel that is exactly ρ-periodic, w(Δ+τ) = ρ·w(Δ), is not standard. Its benefit is not yet separated from the clamp (T3 notes that the bound comes from the clamp).
3. *Asymmetric addressing: sparse DG write keys and a dense direct read query.* The idea is [Treves & Rolls 1992](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450020209)'s, and untied Q/K is standard attention. Combining sparse keys with dense queries in one store is an unusual specific choice, but T16 does not yet test the Treves–Rolls claim (Biological accuracy, point 6).
4. *Self-negative subtractive feedback.* Subtracting an inverted, lagged copy of one's own injection, as opposed to an external negative prompt, is predictive-coding subtraction ([Srinivasan et al. 1982](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1982.0085)) applied to steering. The framing is new; the operation is not.

### Unclear

Error-only injection (removing the component of the injection along h_t) and the norm-relative clamp are reasonable engineering choices with scattered precedent in steering work. I could not find a primary source that establishes them, so I make no novelty call.

## Biological accuracy issues

1. **The docs deny a CA1→CA3 pyramidal back-projection that tracing has found.** `docs/design/temporal-loop.md` states that "CA1 pyramidal cells do not project back to CA3" and grounds the op-amp in Sik's interneurons alone. [Lin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001127) report excitatory projections from ventral CA1 (and subiculum) to dorsal CA3 excitatory neurons, and [Lin et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.25510) show these inputs also reach CA3 interneurons. The biological feedback is mixed in sign and runs across the septotemporal axis (ventral to dorsal), so "an inverted copy" is a design choice, not an anatomical fact. The op-amp analogy should be presented as an engineering abstraction motivated by [Sik et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8085161) and [Jinno et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.1847-07.2007), with the excitatory component acknowledged.
2. **The subtractive feedback is not wired onto CA3.** In `HippocampalLoop._inject`, β·ȳ is subtracted from Δ at the injection summing junction. Keys, values, relevance and stored content are untouched. With the default `loop="open"`, the injection never enters the store, so the only loop is around the injector itself. Back-projection cells, by contrast, inhibit CA3 and the hilus and so shape retrieval. A biologically faithful variant would let the feedback suppress CA3 retrieval of recently re-injected content (for example by down-weighting relevance a(t,s) for recently injected s), which is also the mechanism that could address primacy (T11, T15).
3. **CA2 sits in series on the Schaffer path.** `HippocampalBlock` applies the CA2 FiLM to CA3's output before CA1. Anatomically, Schaffer collaterals reach CA1 directly; CA2 is a parallel EC-II-driven station with strong output to CA1 ([Chevaleyre & Siegelbaum 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.04.013)). Running CA2 in parallel (EC→CA2→CA1, combined at CA1) would match anatomy and would make CA2 ablations separable from CA3 ablations.
4. **The theta analogy does not match theta.** The `osc` kernel's phase is a function of *lag* t−s, so items stored at different times are at different phases at any given time. In [Hasselmo et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976602317318965), encoding and retrieval alternate on a *global* phase shared by all items. Theta sequences are forward-sweeping, compressed look-ahead within each cycle ([Foster & Wilson 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20345); [Kay et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.014)), not periodic re-expression of content stored τ steps ago with decaying amplitude. "Nothing returns within one circuit" is a refractory delay, not phase separation. The closest biological reading of the kernel is big-loop re-entry through EC ([Koster et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.08.009)) or simple delay-line reverberation.
5. **The time channel models entorhinal temporal context, not hippocampal time cells.** `TimeChannel` computes L_j(t) = (1−ρ_j)Σρ_j^{t−s}u_s, the Laplace-domain representation. Shankar & Howard's time cells come from an approximate *inverse* of it, which yields unimodal, sequentially tiled units ([Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00212); [Howard et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.5808-12.2014)). The leaky-integrator features match LEC temporal context cells ([Tsao et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0459-6)) and monkey EC ([Bright et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917197117)). Hippocampal time cells ([MacDonald et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.07.012); [Cao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.75353)) would need the inverse stage. The docs should say "entorhinal temporal-context code".
6. **The block has no EC-II→CA3 direct path, and T16 does not test Treves–Rolls.** In `HippocampalBlock`, CA3's queries come only from the DG code through the mossy projection, which is exactly the arrangement [Treves & Rolls 1992](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450020209) argued impairs retrieval; the direct read exists only in `HippocampalLoop`. In the loop, the T16 comparison (`read=direct` versus `read=dg`, both with `dg_keys=True`) changes two things at once: dense versus sparse query, and an *untied* projection W_q versus the *same* projection W_k used for the keys. A tied k-WTA read of a noisy cue is naturally robust, so the DG read winning (0.94 against 0.84 at σ = 2) is expected. Treves and Rolls' argument concerns a recurrent attractor whose mossy input is a strong, effectively random "detonator" at storage; it is not about a feedforward lookup with a learned, tied code.
7. **The comparator is mistimed by one step.** With `sequential=True`, a query at position t retrieves the stored *successor* of the best-matching cue, which is a prediction of position t+1. `HippocampalBlock` then compares this with the current EC input x_t. Within a segment the working keys and values are autoassociative (v = W_v h_t), so one softmax mixes "what comes next" (store) with "what is here" (segment). A temporal prediction-error unit ([Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.05.024)) should compare the prediction made at t−1 with the input at t. Because the novelty head is supervised on token loss, it can learn surprise from x_t alone and hide the misalignment; this needs a direct test (E1).
8. **EC-II and EC-III are the same tensor.** Both the DG input and the temporoammonic input are x. That is a defensible simplification, but the island/ocean dissociation ([Kitamura et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1244634)) suggests a cheap improvement: two learned projections of x with separate roles.

## Proposed experiments

**E1. Time-aligned CA1 comparator (priority H; extends M1, M2, J3).** *Hypothesis:* comparing CA3's successor prediction from t−1 with EC at t produces a novelty signal that tracks surprisal better than the current same-step comparison, and improves the novelty-gated write. *Design:* on `assoc_recall` and `continual`, three arms: (a) current block; (b) shifted comparator, r̂_t = retrieved_{t−1}; (c) comparator removed, novelty head on x_t only. Same `novelty_alignment_loss`, 3 seeds. Additionally, train arm (b) without the alignment loss to see whether the comparator alone carries surprise. *Baseline:* arm (c). *Metrics:* Spearman correlation of novelty with held-out token surprisal; AUROC for top-decile-loss tokens; binding recall beyond the window (M1 metric); fraction of writes that land on query-relevant tokens.

**E2. Trisynaptic versus monosynaptic double dissociation (H; extends M1, M7).** *Hypothesis:* following [Nakashiba et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1151120) and [Schapiro et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0049), the CA3 route is needed for one-shot binding recall and the TA route suffices for statistical regularities. *Design:* zero the Schaffer input to CA1 (r := 0) or the TA input (a := 0), at training and at evaluation only. Tasks: (i) one-shot key–value binding beyond the attention window (existing `assoc_recall`); (ii) a statistical-learning stream (tokens generated from a community-structured graph as in Schapiro et al.), scored by next-token loss on transitions seen many times. *Baseline:* full block and windowed attention. *Metric:* recall on (i), excess loss on (ii) relative to the graph entropy; the prediction is a crossover interaction.

**E3. The waterbed test of subtractive feedback (H; extends T6, T7, T10, S4).** *Hypothesis:* because the open-loop transfer L(z) = K(1−ρ)z^{−τ}/(1−ρz^{−1}) is stable and strictly proper, Bode's sensitivity integral forces the mean of log|S| over frequency to be zero. Attenuating persistent echo must therefore amplify some band. For τ = 16, ρ = 0.9 I computed |S| = 0.50 at DC but a peak of 2.51 at a period of about 46 tokens for K = 1 (1.44 at K = 0.5; 9.6 at K = 1.5), with about 48% of frequencies amplified. This may explain T10 (subtractive feedback failed to bound echo: 1.7–2.6×). *Design:* in `experiments/loop_systems.py`, drive the untrained loop with content periodic at 8–128 tokens and measure the gain at each period against the prediction. Then train what-when with events spaced near the resonant period against random spacing. Test S4's lead–lag compensation with the same tool: compensation can move the amplified band but cannot remove it. *Baseline:* `feedback=none`, `divisive`. *Metric:* measured |S(ω)| against theory; echo ratio and what-accuracy by event spacing.

**E4. A proper Treves–Rolls test (M; extends T16, M7, W4).** *Hypothesis:* a direct dense read beats a DG read when the DG code is a high-gain, effectively random expansion and retrieval is by recurrent completion. *Design:* a 2×2 of query projection (tied W_k versus untied W_q) by key code (dense versus k-WTA sparse), plus a third factor, DG as a learned versus a fixed random expansion. Use `CA3Autoassociative` with n_iter ∈ {1, 3}; sweep stored items (8–64) and cue noise σ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}; 3 seeds. *Baseline:* the current T16 arms. *Metric:* exact recall against load and noise; the prediction is that the direct-read advantage appears only in the fixed-random, high-load cells.

**E5. Global theta gate versus lag-locked kernel (M; extends T1, T5, T11–T15, S8).** *Hypothesis:* a Hasselmo-style global phase gate, writing on phase φ(t) ∈ [0, π) and reading on [π, 2π) with φ(t) = 2πt/τ, reduces self-rehearsal primacy more than the lag kernel, because injected content is never stored in the same phase as it is read. *Design:* replace W[t,s] with g_read(t)·g_write(s)·ρ^{(t−s)/τ}; run the closed-loop what-when and recency probes. *Baseline:* `osc`, `exp`. *Metric:* older/newer naming ratio (T11 metric), what/when accuracy, echo ratio.

**E6. Time-channel baselines (M; extends T1, W2).** *Hypothesis:* the leaky-integrator clock gains little over gated counting or learned-pole SSMs at equal state size, but an inverse-Laplace (time-cell) readout generalises to unseen gaps. *Design:* arms with equal feature counts: (a) `TimeChannel`; (b) CoPE-style gated cumulative count ([Golovneva et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18719)); (c) diagonal LRU with learned complex poles ([Orvieto et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349)); (d) `TimeChannel` followed by a fixed Post-approximation inverse ([Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00212)). Train on gaps 20–140 and test on 140–400. *Metric:* "when" accuracy in-range and out-of-range; Weber-like scaling of when-error with gap.

**E7. Feedback-Transformer and value-residual baselines (M; extends T1, T3, P4, M1).** *Hypothesis:* at matched parameters and attention windows, the regulated temporal loop beats a Feedback-Transformer-style memory ([Fan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09402)) on what-when and on echo control, and the CA1 comparator beats a plain value residual ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17897)) on binding recall. *Design:* swap the loop for (i) a feedback memory that averages the upper-layer state into the tap's KV memory; and swap the block's CA1 for (ii) the residual a + λr with λ learned. *Metric:* what/when accuracy, echo ratio, and recall beyond the window. A negative result here would mean the hippocampal framing adds nothing over these baselines.

**E8. Sign-agnostic back-projection (L; extends T9, T15, S5).** *Hypothesis:* if β is learned and unconstrained, training settles on negative (inhibitory) feedback for content memory, as the op-amp story predicts. The mixed-sign anatomy ([Lin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001127)) allows it not to. *Design:* parameterise β ∈ ℝ per channel group, with the clamp removed; feed the feedback either into the injector (current) or into relevance suppression (biology point 2). *Metric:* learned sign distribution, what-accuracy, primacy ratio.

## Open questions

The track has shown, at 0.1-million-parameter scale, that subtractive feedback obeys the closed-loop gain law and rings at the predicted critical gain. It has not shown that any hippocampal ingredient of the loop (lag kernel, contrast, back-projection) beats the obvious ML baselines at equal compute. E3 and E7 are the cheapest ways to settle that. It is also unresolved whether CA1's job in a transformer should be a *comparator* that emits novelty, as in `ca1.py`, or a *plasticity gate* that uses the EC-III input as a local target, as the plateau and BTSP literature suggest ([Grienberger & Magee 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05378-6); [Bittner et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan3846)). The second reading points toward a local auxiliary loss in which CA3's t−1 prediction is trained to match EC at t, which is essentially [Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.05.024) inside a transformer layer. Finally, the results on frozen Qwen3.5 (P2) route recall through the GDN layers above the tap, so in that setting the "CA3" may be the host model's own GDN stack rather than the side store. If so, the CA1/feedback machinery should be evaluated as a controller of the host's recurrent memory, not as a memory in its own right, and the loop-gain analysis then applies to the host–side-channel composite. Most results in this track are single-seed (REGISTER), so every difference of a few points above should be treated as provisional until O2 and S3 are run.
