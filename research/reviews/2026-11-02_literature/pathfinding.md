# Pathfinding through remembered contexts: Samsonovich & Ascoli (2005) and limbic-llm

Samsonovich and Ascoli argued that the hippocampus does more than reactivate a pointer to an episode. It finds a short path through a graph of remembered contexts, so that the context an episode needs in order to be understood is reinstated step by step ([Samsonovich & Ascoli 2005](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.85205)). Two separable pieces carry the model. One is a CA3→CA3 store of transitions between contexts. The other is a CA3→CA1 goal field written during sharp waves, which the search climbs greedily. limbic-llm already has the first piece, in its successor-bound CA3 store and its tied/DG Gated DeltaNet addressing. That piece is long-established prior art, not a contribution. The repository does not have the second piece, and it has no task that would need it. Twenty years of descendants recast this computation as successor representations, hindsight goal relabelling, replay-as-planning and graph search over replay buffers. These descendants make the 2005 model's machine-learning content easy to state: a goal-conditioned temporal-distance table learned in hindsight, used for greedy best-first search over stored transitions. They also point to the experiment limbic-llm is missing: multi-hop recall where the answer is reachable only through a bridge binding that never co-occurs with the query.

## Scope

This review covers the attached paper, its lineage, and its descendants in neuroscience and machine learning, through November 2026. It maps the paper onto the limbic-llm repository at commit 3799b36 (`limbic_llm/ca3.py`, `gated_delta.py`, `replay.py`, `hippocampal_block.py`, `ca2.py`, `temporal_loop.py`, and `results/evolve/REGISTER.md`). It also covers hippocampus-to-deep-learning architectures that the repository's `REFERENCES.md` does not list. It does not re-review the CA2 alignment controller or the feedback-stability programme, except where they bear on pathfinding. Forward citations of the 2005 paper were enumerated from OpenAlex (128 citing records). One fact is worth stating at the outset: the landmark descendants discussed below do not appear among those records. Samsonovich and Ascoli's lineage is therefore intellectual rather than bibliographic, and the field mostly reinvented their mechanism without citing it. This absence rests on one index and should be read as provisional.

## The 2005 model

### Architecture and learning

The hippocampal module is a two-layer network of N CA3 units and N CA1 units in one-to-one correspondence. Each unit stands for a group of principal cells representing one previously explored abstract context, defined after McCarthy as a set of rules and facts that applies to a class of situations. The anatomical motivation is quantitative and drawn from the rodent literature: about 3×10^5 CA3 and 4×10^5 CA1 pyramidal cells; roughly 0.1% of CA3 and 1% of CA1 cells active at any location; about 12,000 recurrent CA3 inputs per CA3 cell and 16,000 Schaffer and commissural inputs per CA1 cell. Two plastic matrices carry the computation. The CA3→CA3 matrix J stores the connections of the cognitive graph, meaning the transitions between contexts that have actually been traversed. In the episodic simulations, J stores only transitions that succeeded in switching a separate neocortical attractor network from one stored pattern to another. The CA3→CA1 matrix W stores goal associations, with self-connections fixed at W_ii = 2.

W is written during sharp waves. During exploration the model pauses at arbitrary moments. The current location is taken as a potential future goal, and recently active CA3 units are reactivated with a strength that falls off with how long ago they last fired. Each reactivated CA3 unit j is then bound to the goal's CA1 unit i by W_ij ← max(W_ij, 1/(t − t_j)). The authors state that the exact form does not matter, so long as it decreases monotonically with elapsed time. They also note that the rule is Q-learning or a TD update in all but name. They decline the reinforcement-learning label because the goal is chosen after the fact rather than given in advance. In current terms, this is hindsight goal relabelling ([Andrychowicz et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495)), proposed twelve years earlier. Because of the max, W[G, j] holds the reciprocal of the shortest observed number of steps from j to G. It is a sampled, optimistic goal-conditioned distance table.

### Search dynamics and capacity

Retrieval reuses a reinterpretation of theta phase precession. On each theta cycle the CA3 activity packet sweeps in one candidate direction, or along one stored efferent connection of J. A goal module "listens" to the CA1 unit of the goal, and a prefrontal control module commits to the candidate that excites that unit most. If no candidate excites it, the step is random. The search therefore climbs W toward the goal along edges of J. The model was tested in four settings. In a continuous two-dimensional maze it reached a goal after one random exploration with paths about 60 times shorter than the exploration walk. In the 27-state three-disc Tower of Hanoi, solving time fell sharply once exploration exceeded about 100 moves, which is the point at which the goal state had almost certainly been visited. In a five-attractor episodic example, retrieving episode E directly from state A failed, but the route A→B→D→E succeeded. This is the paper's concrete counter-example to classical index theory: the right pointer is sometimes insufficient without the right path. The fourth test is a large random network.

In that network, each of N units has 10 random efferents, and sharp-wave epochs are simulated as backward walks of length M along J. At N = 10^4, n = 10 and M = 5, retrieval paths averaged 6.75 ± 3.10 steps against 4.22 ± 0.68 for the shortest path. All 10^4 trajectories were shorter than 60 steps, against roughly 7,000 for a random walk. Gaussian noise of 2.5% of peak CA1 activity lengthened paths by 9%. Doubling N lengthened them by 30% while the shortest path grew by 7%. Shortening M from 5 to 2 cost 11%, and M beyond 100 gave no gain. Networks of up to 30,000 contexts were simulated. The 10^4 "realistic" episodic load comes from the rule of thumb that an attractor network stores about as many patterns as each neuron has synapses. The authors suggest that most long-term memory is semantic and therefore does not depend on the hippocampus.

### Predictions and how they fared

The paper lists six predictions. The two lesion predictions remain untested as far as this review found: that hippocampal or prefrontal damage should selectively impair cued recall of unusual episodes needing other episodic context, and that disconnecting a cluster of pointers should make the whole cluster inaccessible until any one member is recalled. The spatial predictions have fared unevenly.

The strongest confirmation concerns theta. Samsonovich and Ascoli assumed that successive theta cycles explore alternative feasible actions. They conceded that in 2005 no direct evidence existed. Hippocampal theta sequences were later shown to reflect the animal's current goal ([Wikenheiser & Redish 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3909)). Kay and colleagues then showed that hippocampal representations cycle between possible future paths on alternate theta cycles at about 8 Hz ([Kay et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.014)). That is close to the fan-out structure of the paper's Fig. 1F and to prediction 6.

The prediction about place-field shape fared worse. The model locates path information in the gradients of place-cell firing, which should follow shortest explored path distance rather than Euclidean distance and should break at thin barriers. Later work moved path information out of the place fields and into sequences. CA1 place cells did not track changes in the connectivity of a four-box environment that the rats demonstrably knew ([Duvelle et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.005)). In a maze with repeatedly reconfigured barriers, place fields stayed largely stable while replay sequences rerouted around the new barriers ([Widloski & Foster 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.02.002)). The computational claim survives: hippocampal sequences respect learned connectivity and lead to goals. The specific locus, static firing-rate gradients, does not. The backward skew of place fields after unidirectional running (prediction 5) is also explained by the successor representation ([Stachenfeld et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650)), which therefore offers a competing account of the same data.

### A re-implementation check

The large-network simulation was re-implemented from the Methods text to check that the numbers can be reproduced and to obtain a baseline for limbic-llm ([sa2005_replicate.py](scripts/sa2005_replicate.py), [sa2005_replication.json](scripts/sa2005_replication.json)). With N = 10^4, n = 10, M = 5 and 10^4 random start–goal pairs on one random graph, retrieval paths averaged 6.69 ± 3.29 steps against a shortest path of 4.24 ± 0.66. 99.3% of trajectories were shorter than 20 steps and 99.98% shorter than 60. This agrees closely with the published 6.75 ± 3.10 and 4.22 ± 0.68. Doubling N to 2×10^4 gave 9.18 ± 6.02 steps (+37%), with the shortest path at 4.54 (+7%, matching the paper exactly). Two sensitivities did not match. Shortening M to 2 cost 31% rather than 11%. Noise cost 3% rather than 9%, but the noise model here is an approximation because the paper's normalisation is under-specified. Two ablations are new. A random walk reached the goal within 2,000 steps in only 21% of 300 sessions. Removing the 0.2-weighted tail of the CA3 packet, so that each candidate is scored only by its own W entry and not its efferents', lengthened paths fourfold to 28.3 ± 29.8 steps. The two-hop lookahead given by a broad CA3 activity packet is therefore load-bearing. This is directly relevant to limbic-llm, whose DG k-WTA codes make CA3 addresses deliberately narrow. These are single-graph, single-seed numbers and should be read as provisional.

## Closest prior work

### Lineage before 2005

The paper grows out of three bodies of work. The first is Samsonovich and McNaughton's continuous-attractor model of path integration ([Samsonovich & McNaughton 1997](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.17-15-05900.1997)). It supplied the "chart", the population-code reading of phase precession, and the idea of the hippocampus as an internal cognitive model with multiple maps. The second is the cognitive-graph proposal ([Muller, Stead & Pach 1996](https://doi.org/10.1085/jgp.107.6.663)), in which place cells are nodes and potentiated CA3 recurrent synapses are edges whose weights encode distance, so that path planning becomes a graph problem. Samsonovich and Ascoli kept the graph but moved the distance information from the recurrent edges into the CA3→CA1 goal field. The third is the memory-indexing theory ([Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7044.100.2.147)), later updated ([Teyler & Rudy 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20350)) and recast in engram terms ([Goode et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.07.011)). Samsonovich and Ascoli accepted the index theory's division of labour, with pointers in the hippocampus and content in neocortex. They rejected its assumption that reactivating a pointer suffices for retrieval. McNaughton's path-integration account ([McNaughton et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.199.1.173)) supplied the activity statistics and the Euclidean-map hypothesis that their barrier prediction was meant to falsify. limbic-llm cites none of these four sources, although its CA3 store is described in the vocabulary they introduced.

### Replay, prediction and planning

The successor representation is the most consequential reformulation. Dayan introduced it as a TD-learnable matrix of expected discounted future occupancy ([Dayan 1993](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1993.5.4.613)). Stachenfeld and colleagues proposed that place cells encode it ([Stachenfeld et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650)). The SR and the S&A goal field are close relatives. Both are goal-indexed predictive maps, and both support navigation by greedy ascent on M(·, G). They differ in the operator. The SR averages over the behaviour policy with a discount. S&A's max rule keeps the best observed distance. The S&A field is therefore policy-independent and optimistic, but it is not a proper expectation, and it cannot unlearn a shortcut that a new barrier has closed. That weakness matters for continual learning in limbic-llm.

Replay moved from consolidation to planning in the same period. Awake reverse replay of the path just taken was reported the year after the paper ([Foster & Wilson 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04587)). It is the empirical counterpart of S&A's backward, recency-weighted binding of recent states to the place where the animal stopped. Replay also includes paths never actually run ([Gupta et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.01.034)). Awake sequences before a trial depict the future path to a remembered goal ([Pfeiffer & Foster 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12112)). A normative account derives which memories to replay from their gain and need ([Mattar & Daw 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0232-z)). A recurrent meta-RL agent whose prefrontal network learns when to spend time on rollouts reproduces both human thinking times and replay statistics ([Jensen, Hennequin & Mattar 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-024-01675-7)). That agent is the modern form of S&A's prefrontal control module. Sharp-wave ripples also act on the hippocampus itself: ripples during learning stabilise the place-cell map ([Roux et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4543)), and ripple content selects which experiences are kept ([Yang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk8261)). This supports S&A's use of sharp waves as moments when hippocampal associations are written, not only moments when hippocampal content is transferred to cortex. Buzsáki's review, which cites the 2005 paper, frames the sharp-wave ripple as a substrate for both episodic memory and planning ([Buzsáki 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22488)).

### Graph-structured hippocampal models

Three recent model families inherit the "map as graph of contexts" idea and improve on S&A in learning the graph rather than assuming one unit per context. The Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine factorises structure from sensory content and generalises relational structure across environments ([Whittington et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024)). Clone-structured cognitive graphs learn context-specific clones of aliased observations and plan by inference on the learned graph ([George et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-22559-5)). The same group later argued that spatial maps are an emergent property of latent higher-order sequence learning ([Raju et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adm8470)). Vector-HaSH stores items by heteroassociation with a fixed grid-cell scaffold of error-correcting states. It separates the pattern-completing address dynamics from content, which avoids the capacity "memory cliff" of Hopfield-type models and supports sequence memory along scaffold trajectories ([Chandra et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08392-y)). The scaffold is the index that S&A and Teyler both posited, now with a capacity analysis. The closest direct descendant that does cite S&A is endotaxis ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.84141)). A three-layer circuit learns, during exploration, a goal-specific "virtual odour" that the animal then climbs. That is S&A's goal field and greedy ascent, with a learning rule derived from a map layer of recurrent connections.

### Memory and path search in language models

The 2005 paper's central claim has a precise counterpart in current language-model work: the answer is retrievable only after an intermediate, unmentioned context has been reinstated. Latent multi-hop reasoning in LLMs is partial. Models often resolve the first hop to the bridge entity but do not reliably use it for the second ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16837)). When they do, the bridge is resolved too late in depth for the second hop to use it ([Biran et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12775)). Fine-tuned models fail to compose two synthetic facts latently, although they succeed when one fact is natural ([Balesni, Korbak & Evans 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353), preprint). Transformers learn implicit composition only through grokking and do not generalise it out of distribution ([Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)).

Explicit search fares little better without help. In a systematic evaluation, LLMs hallucinated invalid trajectories and got trapped in loops on graph-planning tasks ([Momennejad et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15129), preprint). Next-token training can fail outright on path-finding in path-star graphs because of teacher forcing ([Bachmann & Nagarajan 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963)). Small transformers can learn graph connectivity, expanding reachable sets layer by layer, but learning gets harder as graphs grow, and neither scale nor chain-of-thought fixes it ([Saparov et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04703)). Externalised search does help: training on A* traces ([Lehnert et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14083), preprint) and deliberate search over a tree of thoughts ([Yao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601)) both work. Language models can nonetheless build a cognitive map in context. Given random walks on a grid whose nodes are familiar words, their representations abruptly reorganise to match the graph ([Park et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00070)).

On the memory side, HippoRAG cites index theory explicitly ([Gutiérrez et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831)). HippoRAG 2 extends its personalised-PageRank spread over an LLM-built knowledge graph and reports a 7% gain on associative-memory tasks over the best embedding retriever ([Gutiérrez et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)). PageRank diffuses activation in one shot from the query entities. It does not search for a path to a goal, so it implements the index without the pathfinder. EM-LLM segments context into events by surprise ([Fountas et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09450)), and Larimar adds a writable episodic memory to an LLM ([Das et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11901)). Neither chains retrievals. The work closest to limbic-llm's own agenda is a 2026 preprint by Lee and colleagues ([Lee et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099), preprint). Before the KV cache is cleared, the model runs N offline recurrent "sleep" passes over the accumulated context, writing it into the fast weights of SSM blocks through a learned local rule. Longer sleep helps most on multi-hop graph retrieval and other deep-reasoning examples. That work combines three of limbic-llm's ingredients: offline consolidation, fast-weight recurrent memory in a hybrid model, and freeing the KV cache.

### Other hippocampus-to-deep-learning architectures

Several architectures not in `REFERENCES.md` bear on this track. Neural Episodic Control keeps a fast-written, semi-tabular value memory alongside slow representations ([Pritzel et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01988)). MERLIN couples a predictive memory to a goal-directed agent ([Wayne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10760)). The Differentiable Neural Computer is the most direct machine-learning anticipation of successor-bound memory. Its temporal link matrix records the order of writes, so a read can step forward or backward through stored sequences, and it was used for graph traversal and shortest-path questions ([Graves et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20101)). Search on the Replay Buffer builds a graph whose nodes are stored observations and whose edge weights come from a goal-conditioned value function, then plans by graph search ([Eysenbach, Salakhutdinov & Levine 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05253)). That is S&A's large-network simulation with learned distances.

Inside transformers, induction heads implement [A][B]…[A]→[B] by pairing a previous-token head with a copying head ([Olsson et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895)). That is successor-bound lookup implemented by attention. Long Sequence Hopfield Memory extends temporally asymmetric Hebbian sequence networks to dense associative capacity ([Chaudhry et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04532)). A recent review gives the key–value framing of biological memory that limbic-llm relies on ([Gershman, Fiete & Irie 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02950), accepted at Neuron).

Several hippocampal transformers appeared in 2026. Hippoformer, which is in the repository's bibliography, is a TEM descendant: an "mm-TEM" with a meta-learned MLP relational memory, joined to a transformer working memory and evaluated on 2D and 3D spatial prediction ([Li et al. 2026](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/li2026iclr-hippoformer/)). HippoTune embeds iterative query–retrieve–feedback loops in every transformer layer for continual fine-tuning. Restricting it to a single retrieval step costs accuracy ([HippoTune, ICLR 2026](https://openreview.net/forum?id=MtDiLnnYgm)). It is iterated CA3 retrieval without a goal. An actor-critic agent with a hippocampus-inspired intrinsic sequence generator navigates a maze from sparse egocentric input, and its performance depends on sequence length ([Lin, Yiu & Leibold 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09951), ICLR 2026).

Some names mislead. HiPPO is an optimal polynomial-projection framework for online memory ([Gu et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07669)), with no hippocampal content. It is nevertheless an ancestor of the state-space line that leads to the Gated DeltaNet layers limbic-llm uses. Nominally "hippocampal" agent-memory systems such as Hippo, which consolidates agent reflections by analogy to replay ([Song et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24453), preprint), borrow the vocabulary but not the circuit.

## Assessment of limbic-llm

### Novel versus anticipated

The successor-bound CA3 store is anticipated. `CA3Autoassociative(sequential=True)` stores h_t ↦ h_{t+1}, and the tied/DG GDN modes bind a_{t−1} ↦ v_t. The repository attributes this to Levy ([Levy 1996](https://doi.org/10.1002/%28sici%291098-1063%281996%296:6%3C579::aid-hipo3%3E3.0.co;2-c)) and to Jensen and Lisman ([Jensen & Lisman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.3.2-3.279)), correctly. The same binding is S&A's J matrix, the DNC temporal link matrix, the induction-head circuit, and the asymmetric Hopfield sequence memory. The repository's own `gated_delta.py` docstring notes that standard GDN's short causal convolution already "supplies the token shift that binds a key to the value that follows it". The `tied` mode makes that shift explicit and shares one address between query and key. The "exact RAM with one-hot keys" observation is also anticipated. It is the delta rule with orthogonal keys ([Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)), the NTM/DNC location-addressing idea, and the Qwenputer blog the repository already cites. What limbic-llm adds is evidence, not mechanism: the capacity collapse of standard GDN past its key size and the perfect recall to 32 pairs with successor-bound keys (REGISTER M4, M5), on single-seed CPU models.

Replay with sleep consolidation of slow parameters is anticipated by complementary learning systems theory ([McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)) and its continual-learning implementations. For the specific combination of offline passes into the fast weights of a hybrid model, it is anticipated by Lee et al. (2026). Novelty as a replay priority is one half of Mattar and Daw's gain-times-need. The M3 result (backward transfer −0.73 to 0.00) has no uniform-replay control yet (O1), so the priority itself is untested.

Pathfinding is absent, and where others have built it, it is anticipated. Nothing in limbic-llm searches toward a goal. The CA3 `n_iter` loop re-anchors each query to the cue (`xi = h + retrieved`), so iterations neither chain successors nor settle into an attractor. Goal-directed search over stored transitions is published three times over: in the 2005 paper itself, in the DNC and in SoRB. Putting a goal field into a frozen LLM's recurrent side memory and testing it on multi-hop recall beyond the attention window would be a new combination. The parts are not new.

The frozen-model side-channel results are the most original material in the repository. P2 restores exact needle recall 500 tokens back on Qwen3.5 when attention is windowed, through the GDN layers above the tap. The nearest neighbours are Larimar, EM-LLM, Lee et al. (2026) and HippoTune. None of them reports that a hybrid's own GDN layers relay an injected memory to the output while its attention is blinded. This is a mechanistic claim about Qwen3.5 worth tightening with the ablations already planned (O5). It is a single-seed result.

The temporal loop is partially novel as engineering. Its time channel follows established time-cell theory ([Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00212); [Howard & Kahana 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmps.2001.1388)). Its damped-oscillatory re-injection resembles the theta sweep of S&A, but it injects one retrieved value rather than alternating candidates, so it cannot do the exploration that the 2005 paper and Kay et al. (2020) attribute to theta. Big-loop recurrence through the hippocampus as a means of multi-hop inference is anticipated by REMERGE ([Kumaran & McClelland 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028681)) and its human imaging test ([Koster et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.08.009)), both already cited.

### Biological accuracy issues

The Strategy I block wires CA2 in series between CA3 and CA1 (`DG → CA3 → CA2(FiLM) → CA1`), so all retrieved CA3 content passes through the CA2 modulator. Anatomically, Schaffer collaterals carry CA3 output to CA1 directly. CA2 is a parallel node. Cell-type-specific tracing found that dentate granule cells send functional monosynaptic input to CA2, and that CA2 projects preferentially to deep-layer CA1, forming a DG→CA2→CA1-deep circuit parallel to DG→CA3→CA1 ([Kohara et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3614)). The same paper contradicts the `ca2.py` docstring's statement that "CA2 lacks mossy-fibre input". A biologically faithful wiring would sum a CA3→CA1 path with a CA2 path converging on a separate "deep" CA1 sub-population. The repository's CA2-as-social-context-selector reading ([Hitti & Siegelbaum 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13028)) is compatible with either wiring.

The episodic store holds content vectors, not pointers. In both the index theory and S&A, the hippocampus stores sparse pointers, and the content lives in neocortex. Vector-HaSH makes the same separation explicit. limbic-llm's CA3 slots hold full residual-stream vectors, which makes them functionally a selective KV cache. That is a defensible engineering choice, but it should not be described as hippocampal indexing. If the DG code were the stored key and the residual stream the value, as in the GDN `dg` mode, the mapping would be closer.

Sharp waves are mapped only onto cortical consolidation. `replay.py` frames sharp-wave replay as rehearsal for training slow parameters, and replays whole token sequences forward. Biologically, sharp waves also write and stabilise hippocampal associations (S&A 2005; Roux et al. 2017; Yang et al. 2024). Replay runs in reverse after reward (Foster & Wilson 2006), constructs never-experienced paths (Gupta et al. 2010), and depicts routes to goals before they are run (Pfeiffer & Foster 2013; Widloski & Foster 2022). The model therefore lacks the planning role of replay, and the 2005 paper shows that role can be implemented with a single extra matrix.

The CA3 iteration does not do what its docstring says. "Recurrent settling" with `xi = h + retrieved` is a residual refinement anchored to the cue. With a heteroassociative store, a true recurrent iteration should advance along the stored sequence, which is what Jensen and Lisman's CA3 model does. As written, `n_iter > 1` retrieves the successor of a cue-plus-successor mixture.

## Proposed experiments

Six experiments follow, in priority order. Each extends one or more REGISTER entries.

**Missing-key multi-hop recall (H; extends M1, M5, W1, P4a).** The hypothesis is that single-read memory modules solve only one hop, and that chaining k reads through a successor-bound memory restores k-hop recall beyond the window with accuracy near p^k, where p is single-hop accuracy. Full and windowed attention degrade with k, consistent with the two-hop literature. Generate sequences containing chains key→bridge_1→…→value, with every binding placed outside a 16-token window and the bridge tokens never adjacent to the query. The query gives the head key and asks for the k-th link, k = 1 to 6. Arms: full attention; windowed attention; Titans; GDN `tied` and `dg` with one read; the same GDN with k chained reads (q_{i+1} = address(o_i)); and the CA3 store with `n_iter` changed so the query becomes the retrieved successor. Report exact match against k, per-hop accuracy, and the M5-style counterfactual (swap one bridge binding and check that predictions follow). On Qwen3.5, place two needles A→B and B→C 500 and 1,000 tokens back with attention windowed, and compare one against two reads of the W1 side memory.

**Goal-field path search (H; extends M5, M3, O1).** The hypothesis is that a second delta-rule matrix written at hindsight "sharp-wave" events turns the successor memory into a pathfinder that approaches S&A's 1.6× optimality ratio. Present random graphs (N = 10^2 to 10^4, out-degree 10) as random-walk token streams, as in Park et al. (2025). At random moments, write address(j) ↦ goal code for the last M visited states, with strength γ^{t−t_j}. At test time, given start and goal tokens, enumerate the candidate successors of the current node, score each by its goal-field readout plus a 0.2-weighted readout of its own successors, and step greedily. Baselines: BFS optimum; the numpy re-implementation here (6.69 steps against 4.24 shortest at N = 10^4); a small transformer trained on paths (the Bachmann–Nagarajan and Saparov settings); and an SR-learned field. Metrics: success, path length over shortest, invalid edges, loops, and robustness to readout noise. The re-implementation predicts a fourfold penalty without the successor tail. That makes DG sparsity a testable trade-off between interference (T13) and lookahead.

**Replay content and direction (M; extends M3, W3, O1).** The hypothesis is that reverse, goal-anchored replay builds goal fields with fewer replay steps than forward novelty-prioritised replay, and that a gain-times-need priority beats novelty alone. Compare uniform, novelty, reverse-from-salient-event, and gain×need priorities. For need, use the SR occupancy from the current state; for gain, use the loss reduction. Metrics: backward transfer on the continual suite and goal-field path-search success after a fixed sleep budget.

**Barrier reconfiguration (M; extends M5, T5).** The hypothesis is that S&A's max rule cannot unlearn a closed shortcut, whereas an SR-style or delta-rule goal field with decay α < 1 can, at the cost of shorter reach. This is the trade-off T5 found for damping. After training on a graph, delete a set of edges, then measure invalid-path rate and re-learning time under max-rule, delta-rule and SR goal fields. This is the machine analogue of Widloski and Foster's barrier experiment.

**Alternating candidates in the loop (M; extends R2, R3, J4, D-b).** The hypothesis is that the temporal loop can implement the theta sweep. Alternate injection of the top-k retrieved candidates over successive loop periods, and let a CA1-style goal score select between them. On chain-of-thought tasks with graph structure (CogEval-style planning, then GSM8K), this should give more accuracy per reasoning token than single-direction gist steering. Report accuracy against tokens, loop and invalid-step rates, and stability under the S1 gain sweep. This arm is positive feedback, so S1 should run first.

**Cluster disconnection (L; extends M5).** S&A predicted that deleting one bridge binding makes a whole downstream cluster unreachable, and that cueing any one member restores the rest. Delete a bridge in the multi-hop task, then measure reachability of every downstream node before and after a single direct cue. This distinguishes a graph-structured store from a flat associative one.

## Open questions

The first open question is enumeration. S&A had explicit units, so the candidate successors of a context could be probed one by one. A delta-rule state holding several successors for one key returns their superposition. limbic-llm needs a mechanism to decompose that readout, through sparse DG codes, sampling, or a vocabulary-level candidate set, before greedy goal ascent is even defined.

The second is what the goal is in language reasoning. In navigation the goal is a place. In multi-hop question answering it may be an answer type or a relation slot, and in chain-of-thought perhaps a verified sub-result. The 2005 paper delegates goal specification and monitoring to prefrontal cortex. limbic-llm's CA1 comparator and the planned calibrated decision head (J1, J4) are the candidates.

The third is whether search should be latent or externalised. Saparov et al. (2024) found that chain-of-thought did not rescue learned search on large graphs. Lehnert et al. (2024) and Yao et al. (2023) found that externalised search traces help. A recurrent side memory that searches internally, as in S&A or Lee et al. (2026), is a third option. Whether it composes with the big-loop recurrence in D-b is unknown.

The fourth is capacity. S&A assumed one pointer per context and about 10^4 contexts. limbic-llm's CA3 store has 256 to 512 slots, and its GDN state is d_k × d_v per head. Vector-HaSH's scaffold suggests a way to decouple the number of addressable contexts from content dimension, but nobody has tried it inside a language model.

The fifth concerns where path information should live. The neuroscience since 2005 places it in sequences and replay rather than in place-field gradients. For limbic-llm, the analogous question is whether the goal field should be a static matrix, as in S&A, or a property of generated sequences, as in replay-as-planning. Only the second can respond to a reconfigured graph without relearning the map.
