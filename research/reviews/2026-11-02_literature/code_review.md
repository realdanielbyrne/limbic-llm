# Technical review of limbic-llm: code, experiments and claims

Repository: `realdanielbyrne/limbic-llm`, commit `3799b36` (merge of PR #2, `feat/temporal-loop`). Reviewed 2 November 2026.

## Scope

This review checks the code in `limbic_llm/` and `experiments/` against the equations it claims to implement, the experiments in `results/` against the conclusions recorded in `RESULTS.md`, `results/evolve/REGISTER.md`, `results/evolve/NOTEBOOK.md` and `results/pretrained_HANDOFF.md`, and both against the published literature. It covers the user's four tracks: CA3–CA1 style skip connections, CA2 side-channel injection for reasoning and alignment, DG–CA3 style sparse experts, and Gated DeltaNet (GDN) modules as RNN-style memory in Qwen models. Every module and experiment script was read. The test suite was run in a fresh environment (PyTorch 2.14.1 CPU, Python 3.11.18). The GDN kernels, the ringing threshold of the subtractive feedback loop and the effect of the neurogenesis gradient boost under Adam were checked with independent code. A binding-free baseline for the associative-recall task was computed by simulation. The alignment and chain-of-thought experiments have no results in the repository, so for those tracks the review covers design only.

The main conclusion is uncomfortable but useful. The repository is carefully engineered, the GDN recurrence and its chunkwise solver are exactly right, and the register is unusually candid about its own confounds. But the headline small-model memory result (M1) and the GDN capacity result (M4) are, on inspection, measurements of a heuristic that needs no key–value binding at all, and the headline pretrained result (P1/P2) is confounded by an untrained, out-of-distribution windowed baseline. What survives is narrower: successor-bound (previous-token) addressing gives exact recall, which is a known mechanism, and a small trained module at mid-depth of a frozen Qwen3.5 restores needle recall, for reasons not yet established.

### Gated DeltaNet

`gated_delta.py` implements the gated delta rule of [Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464), $S_t = \alpha_t S_{t-1}(I-\beta_t k_t k_t^\top) + \beta_t v_t k_t^\top$, both token by token and with a chunkwise solve. The chunkwise form sets up the unit-lower-triangular system $(I+L)U = \mathrm{diag}(\beta)(V-\mathrm{diag}(\Gamma)KS_0^\top)$ with $L_{ti} = \beta_t(\Gamma_t/\Gamma_i)k_t^\top k_i$, which is the same quantity that the WY/UT-transform algorithm of [Yang et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484) computes, solved here with `solve_triangular` rather than the reference blocked kernel. I compared both repository kernels with a literal transcription of the recurrence (separate code, float64, $B=2$, $H=3$, $T=77$, chunk 16). Maximum output error was $8.9\times10^{-16}$ (mild decay, $\alpha\in[0.9,1]$), $4.4\times10^{-16}$ (strong decay, $\alpha\in[10^{-3},0.2]$) and $2.2\times10^{-15}$ ($\alpha=1$); in float32 with chunk 64 it was below $10^{-6}$. The identity in the docstring, that the update equals decay followed by one SGD step of size $\beta_t$ on $\frac12\lVert Sk_t-v_t\rVert^2$, is correct.

Against the flash-linear-attention reference layer the differences are small and documented here for reproducibility. The decay gate is $\alpha=\exp(-\mathrm{softplus}(Wx+b))$ with a single bias of $-5$ ($\alpha\approx0.993$ for every head), whereas the reference uses the Mamba-2 parameterisation $\exp(-e^{A}\,\mathrm{softplus}(Wx+\Delta))$ with per-head spread in $A$ and $\Delta$ ([Dao & Gu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060)). Queries are not scaled by $d_k^{-1/2}$, which is harmless because outputs pass through a per-head RMSNorm. The short causal convolutions are initialised to the identity (current token only); the reference uses PyTorch's random initialisation. That choice matters more than it looks: with identity convolutions the standard `gdn` mode starts with no token shift, and the shift is exactly what associative recall needs (see the validity section). The optional negative-eigenvalue extension ($\beta\in(0,2)$) of [Grazzi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537) is not supported.

### Titans memory

`neural_memory.py` follows the Titans update of [Behrouz et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663) in form, $S_t=\eta_t S_{t-1}-\theta_t\nabla\ell$, $M_t=(1-\alpha_t)M_{t-1}+S_t$, but not in granularity. Within a chunk every gradient is taken at the chunk-start memory (the paper's mini-batch scheme), and then $\eta$ and $\alpha$ are averaged over the chunk and applied once, so momentum and forgetting act per chunk rather than per token; the paper keeps them per token inside the chunk via a parallel scan. Two further departures are not in Titans: a per-sample trust region that rescales each fused update to norm at most 4, and a cap of 0.4 on $\theta$. The q/k/v depthwise convolutions of Titans are absent, and the MAC variant omits the final gating $o_t=y_t\otimes M^*_t(y_t)$. None of this is wrong as engineering, but every Titans number in the repository is a number for this variant. The meta-gradient fix (differentiating with respect to a fresh alias of $M$) is correct and well motivated.

### CA3, DG, CA1, CA2 and subiculum

`ca3.py` implements modern-Hopfield retrieval as masked softmax attention over a slot store plus the current segment, which is the correspondence of [Ramsauer et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217). Two details differ from the description. First, the docstring speaks of iterated settling with tied weights, but the iteration is $\xi\leftarrow h+\mathrm{retrieved}$ with separate value and output projections, a residual attention step rather than the Hopfield update $\xi\leftarrow X\,\mathrm{softmax}(\beta X^\top\xi)$; all experiments use one iteration anyway. Second, in sequential mode the store returns successors while the current-segment keys return their own (autoassociative) values, so one softmax mixes heteroassociative and autoassociative semantics.

The k-winners-take-all in `dentate_gyrus.py` and `gated_delta.kwta` passes gradient only to the winners through `topk`/`scatter`, the standard choice of [Ahmad & Scheinkman 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11257). The neurogenesis mechanism multiplies the gradient of young units by $1+\beta e^{-\mathrm{age}/\tau}$. Under Adam or AdamW, which every experiment uses, a per-parameter gradient rescaling is normalised away: in a direct check, scaling gradients by 5 changed a 50-step AdamW trajectory by $1.2\times10^{-7}$. The boost therefore only acts through its slow time variation, and `grow()` is never called in any experiment, so neurogenesis is untested. The CA1 comparator's features $[a;r;a\odot r;|a-r|]$ are the sentence-matching features of [Conneau et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02364); its novelty head is trained by `novelty_alignment_loss` to predict z-scored token loss, and the time alignment of that loss is correct. FiLM in `ca2.py` is the identity at initialisation as claimed ([Perez et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07871)).

### Temporal loop, clock and feedback

`loop_kernel` implements $w(\Delta)=A\rho^{\Delta/\tau-1}[\cos(2\pi\Delta/\tau)]_+$ for $\Delta\ge\tau$, with index arithmetic in float32 after the bf16 leak (register C4). Because the cosine is clamped, half of every period has zero weight; the handoff correctly notes that the needle lag of 501–503 tokens lands in such a null. Retrieval relevance $\exp(b(\hat q\cdot\hat k-1))$ is un-normalised, so the loop is in effect one extra attention head over the whole past at one layer, with a fixed age kernel, and its memory is $O(T)$ (register C5). The closed-loop chunking by $\tau$ is exact, since nothing younger than $\tau$ can re-enter.

The op-amp analysis holds. For unclamped, unsaturated, unprojected injection, $y=A(\Delta-\beta\bar y)$ with a normalised leaky kernel gives $y=A\Delta/(1+A\beta)$ in steady state. With the default clamp on (`ca1_control=True`) the subtractive term changes only the direction of the injection, because the clamp sets its norm, so the op-amp law does not describe the deployed configuration; RESULTS §4.3 tests it correctly with the clamp removed. Solving $z^{\tau}-\rho_E z^{\tau-1}+K(1-\rho_E)=0$ independently for $\tau=16$, $\rho_E=0.9$ gives a critical gain of $K=1.680$ and a ringing period of 44.1 tokens, matching T7. The time channel implements log-spaced leaky integrators and damped oscillators driven by a learned salience, bounded in $[-1,1]$, as claimed.

### Alignment and reasoning harness

`steering.py` implements the static, conditional and graded arms as described, with a leaky integral $E_t=\lambda E_{t-1}+s_t$ (not normalised, so with $\lambda=0.9$ it saturates at $10s$, and $\tanh(0.5E)$ becomes a near-binary switch after a few harmful tokens). Two practical issues: the gist direction exists in `steering.py` but is not wired into `run.py`, and in graded mode without a probe the bias of $-30$ makes the gain silently zero, so a gist run in graded mode would do nothing. The probe is fitted on `mlabonne/harmful_behaviors` against `harmless_alpaca`, which differ in style and topic as well as harm, and refusals are judged by substring matching.

## Validity of the reported results

### The associative-recall plateau needs no binding

The task in `experiments/tasks.py` studies six key→value pairs (distinct keys, values drawn with replacement from 16), shows 40 filler tokens, then queries all six keys in shuffled order with teacher forcing, so each answered value is visible when the next query is scored. A model that remembers only the multiset of studied values, and removes each value once it has been revealed, needs no binding at all. I simulated the optimal such predictor (predict the most frequent remaining value; 200,000 sequences). Its accuracy by query position is 0.288, 0.307, 0.337, 0.395, 0.531, 1.000, mean **0.476**. The full-attention reference scores 0.485 overall and 0.26, 0.27, 0.31, 0.37, 0.54, 0.82 by position (RESULTS §1); plain Titans scores 0.483, Titans with sequential writes 0.490, and Strategy I 0.414. Strategy I is below the binding-free ceiling; full attention and both Titans variants exceed it by only 0.007–0.014, which is at most 1.6 binomial standard errors of the 3,072 scored queries in each evaluation (SE ≈ 0.009), so they are statistically indistinguishable from it. The full-attention first-query accuracy (0.26) is below the binding-free first-query value (0.288), where elimination cannot help. The per-position profile that RESULTS cites as evidence that the plateau is "not a task ceiling" is the signature of elimination. M1 should therefore read: these models carry the set of studied values across the gap, which windowed attention does not (0.059); there is no evidence that they bind keys to values. Only the successor-tied GDN memories (1.00 and 0.989) exceed the ceiling, and the counterfactual swap test in §3 shows that they really bind.

The same baseline explains the GDN capacity sweep (M4). The binding-free accuracies at 8, 16 and 32 pairs are 0.424, 0.319 and 0.243; `gdn_x8` scores 0.428, 0.320 and 0.222, and `gdn` with 16-dimensional keys scores 0.426 at 8 pairs, a load below its key dimension. Standard GDN did not learn binding at any load within 1,500 steps, so the curve is not a capacity curve, and the README sentence that recall falls "once the number of stored pairs exceeds its key dimension" is not supported. This matters because delta-rule layers are reported to be among the stronger linear-time models at associative recall and in-context retrieval ([Yang et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484); [Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464)), and the multi-query version of the task was formalised precisely to measure this ([Arora et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927)). Undertraining at 48,000 sequences, the identity-initialised convolutions, and the tiny vocabulary (16 values) are all candidate causes. Full attention at 0.485 tells the same story: small attention models are known to form induction heads that implement exactly this lookup ([Olsson et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895)), and here none formed.

M6 ("an exact delta-rule memory beats Titans' deep memory") is confounded beyond the undertraining. `titans_gdn` differs from `titans_seq` in at least four ways at once: linear rather than deep memory, exact per-token rather than chunk-start updates, four memory heads rather than one, and, in sequential mode, a *tied* query/key projection (`wq = wk`), whereas `NeuralMemory` keeps separate `wq` and `wk` that must learn to align. A tied previous-token address is a hard-wired induction head. The comparison needs a factorial ablation before the advantage can be attributed to the delta rule. M2 ("Refuted") and M7 ("Open") inherit the problem: on a task that only tied addressing solves, neither DG writes nor CA1 novelty could have shown a benefit, so M2 should be Open.

Two further points. The CPU windowed baseline still uses sliding windows: three layers of width 16 reach $3\times15=45$ tokens, more than the 41 tokens from the last studied value to the first query, so the C2 fix (block windows) has not been applied to this task, although the baseline did not exploit the leak. And the MQAR protocol of Zoology (many keys, vocabulary in the thousands, a learning-rate sweep, and results reported per model width) is the accepted standard for this question; the present task is too small to separate architectures.

### Continual learning

M3 (replay with sleep removes forgetting, BWT −0.73 to 0.00) is a valid demonstration that replay works, but not of anything specific to novelty priority or sleep. Each task has 8 keys and a 256-sequence buffer whose sequences each contain about 12 key–value pairs, so the buffer holds essentially every fact of every previous task. Uniform replay ([Rolnick et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)), dark experience replay ([Buzzega et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07211)), and sleep-only and awake-only arms are needed before novelty-prioritised sleep can be credited; the register says this, but the status column still reads Supported.

### Temporal loop statistics

The what/when dissociation (T1) is clean and well controlled: windowed attention sits at chance on *what* (0.109) and at the prior on *when* (0.338), and each channel supplies only its own answer. The stability results T3–T8 are properties of the clamp or of a linear delayed loop; T6 and T7 reproduce analytic predictions exactly (verified above), which confirms the implementation rather than a property of trained models, and running them on "3 untrained seeds" adds no evidence. The primacy and recency findings T11–T15 rest on one seed each, on a zero-shot probe with two events that never co-occur in training, and on differences such as 0.61 against 0.84; T15 already shows the two seeds disagreeing (0.54 against 0.38). These should be Open, and the analogy to the psychological primacy effect should be framed as a hypothesis. Training is deterministic (T19), which removes one source of variance but says nothing about seed variance, the one that matters. At the observed run times (13–23 minutes per 3,000-step CPU run) five seeds per configuration is affordable; results should be reported with bootstrap confidence intervals across seeds, following [Agarwal et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264).

### Pretrained needle results

The needle task (`pairs=1`, digit-free random filler) contains exactly one digit string, so recalling it requires no key–value binding: it is single-needle passkey retrieval ([Mohtashami & Jaggi 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16300)), the easiest NIAH variant; [Hsieh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654) found that near-perfect vanilla NIAH accuracy hides large drops once the types and number of needles and the context length grow. The fixed BOS sink is kept visible, which is right ([Xiao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)).

P1 ("Qwen3.5's GDN layers alone do not recall a needle 500 tokens back") is not supported by the evidence. With only the attention layers windowed, the frozen Qwen3.5 baseline has digit loss 4.03 and token accuracy 0.056, far worse than the answer-format floor (1.84 loss, 0.28 accuracy) that a model knowing only the answer format reaches. The windowed model is out of distribution and does not even produce the format, so its 0.00 says nothing about whether the GDN state carries the needle. Then a 4.38M-parameter side module at block 12 restores exact recall (1.00), while the loop's kernel weight at the needle lag is exactly zero and the loop gain barely trained (0.2689 to 0.2697). The most economical reading is that the side module acts as an adapter that repairs the windowing shift, and that the intact GDN layers, which never lost the needle, then deliver it. The layer-22 control does not distinguish this from the register's "re-injection through later GDN layers" story, because an adapter at block 22 cannot repair blocks 12–21. The decisive controls are a trained adapter of the same size with the loop and clock disabled (or a LoRA on block 12), and the same adapter with GDN windowed. This connects to a known result: models with a fixed-size state are provably and empirically limited at copying from context compared with transformers ([Jelassi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)), so the right test of track 4 is multi-key, multi-value retrieval and copying at increasing load, not one four-digit string.

P4 (the horizon kernel lets the loop alone carry needle information on all-windowed models) is supported as stated: digit loss 1.38 and 1.24 against a floor of 1.84, with every layer windowed. P4a's "exactly one digit known" is one decomposition consistent with the mean loss and accuracy, but partial information spread across digits can produce the same two numbers; it is a hypothesis until per-digit logging (C1a) exists. The Qwen3.5 horizon run left the floor only near step 2,700 of 3,000, so it is undertrained, as the handoff says.

P5 (perplexity) is correctly marked Open; the needed control is a parameter-matched adapter trained on WikiText under the same window, plus evaluation restricted to tokens whose predictive context lies beyond the window.

### Statistical power of the planned alignment runs

With `--n-eval 64`, a refusal rate near 0.5 has a 95% binomial half-width of about 12 points; with all 250 safe and 200 unsafe XSTest prompts the half-widths are about 6 and 7 points. Selectivity differences of 10 points between arms, the effect sizes the notebook anticipates, cannot be resolved at 64 prompts.

## Closest prior work

The recurrent-memory side of limbic-llm sits inside a now well-mapped design space. [Schlag et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174) showed that linear attention is a fast-weight programmer and introduced the delta-rule update; [Yang et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484) made it trainable at scale; [Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464) added the gate. Three unifying accounts then cast all of these layers, including Titans, as online optimisation of a regression loss on key–value pairs: test-time training ([Sun et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)), test-time regression ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352), preprint) and the Miras framework ([Behrouz et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13173), preprint). The repository's observation that the GDN update is one gradient step on the Titans loss at the decayed state is therefore a restatement of these papers, not a new link. Production hybrids that interleave delta-rule layers with full attention, such as Qwen3-Next/Qwen3.5 (3:1) and Kimi Linear, whose delta attention extends Gated DeltaNet with finer gating ([Kimi Team 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692), preprint), are the models the pretrained track hooks into.

On recall, [Fu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14052) diagnosed why state-space models fail associative recall and fixed it with an explicit shift that binds each token to its predecessor; [Olsson et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895) found the same two-step circuit (previous-token head, then induction head) in transformers; Zoology ([Arora et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927)) and Based ([Arora et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668)) formalised the multi-query version and the state-size trade-off; and Canon layers ([Allen-Zhu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17351), preprint) show that short horizontal mixing lifts linear attention and GDN. The repository's successor-bound address, in which the key of write $t$ is the address of token $t-1$, is this shift, hard-wired. Its own result (M5) is supported by this literature rather than new to it.

Hippocampal and episodic memory modules for LLMs are a crowded field that the repository's bibliography already covers in part (Larimar, HippoRAG, EM-LLM, HEMA, HiCL, Hippoformer). Closest to the CA3 slot store are kNN memories over past activations ([Wu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08913)) and compressive linear-attention memories inside attention ([Munkhdalai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07143), preprint). Closest to the temporal loop are feedback architectures in which later representations re-enter earlier computation ([Fan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09402); [Hwang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09173), preprint) and attention with an age-dependent down-weighting of scores, either fixed ([Press et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409)) or gated ([Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02130)); the loop's age kernel is a multiplicative bias of exactly this kind, with a clamped cosine. The time channel's learned-salience integrators resemble contextual position encoding, which increments position only on tokens the model selects ([Golovneva et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18719)), and the real-decay recurrences of linear RNNs.

For DG-style sparse experts the precedents are k-winners-take-all layers ([Ahmad & Scheinkman 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11257)), product-key memories ([Lample et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05242)), retrieval over a million tiny experts ([He 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04153), preprint), sparsely gated MoE ([Fedus et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)), sparse distributed memory as a continual learner with top-k activations ([Bricken et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11934)) and, explicitly hippocampal, the DG-gated MoE of HiCL ([Kapoor et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16651), preprint). For CA3–CA1 style skip connections, the deep-learning counterparts are depth-weighted averaging of earlier layers ([Pagliardini et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02622)), value residuals from the first layer ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17897)) and learned hyper-connections ([Zhu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606)); an EC-III→CA1 "temporoammonic" skip that brings the embedding-level stream to a late comparator is a member of this family.

For the CA2 alignment track, the repository's bibliography already contains activation addition, contrastive activation addition, conditional activation steering ([Lee et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907)), the single refusal direction ([Arditi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)), circuit breakers, and a PID controller for steering ([Nguyen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04309), preprint), which is the closest precedent for a feedback-regulated steering gain. Methods that adapt steering intensity per input or during generation also exist: adaptive activation steering for truthfulness ([Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00034), preprint) and dynamic activation composition ([Scalena et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17563)). [Tan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12404) showed that steerability varies widely across inputs and can be brittle out of distribution, which bears directly on the claim that one transplanted refusal direction can re-align an abliterated model. For reasoning, concise-CoT steering vectors ([Azizi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04742), preprint) and SEAL ([Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07986), preprint) are the baselines R2 already names; budget forcing ([Muennighoff et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393)) is the simplest token-budget baseline and should be added.

## Novelty and anticipation

Most of the computational components are anticipated, and the repository should say so plainly. The GDN-as-gradient-step identity, successor-bound addressing, k-WTA pattern separation, DG-gated experts, softmax Hopfield retrieval, the CA1 matching features, FiLM context, replay with distillation into slow parameters ([van de Ven et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17866-2)), and reserve units for neurogenesis ([Draelos et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03770)) all have direct precedents cited above. The value of the hippocampal mapping is organisational: it gives one vocabulary for a set of known mechanisms and suggests which ones to combine. That is a legitimate framing for a position paper but not, by itself, a contribution a reviewer would accept as new.

Three things are plausibly new and worth pursuing. First, the regulated re-injection loop as a design object: treating additive side-channel injection as a feedback system, distinguishing divisive from subtractive inhibition, deriving the delay-limited critical gain, and showing that closed loops rehearse their first input. The individual pieces are textbook control theory and the closest machine-learning precedent is the PID steering controller, but the specific analysis of self-rehearsal and primacy in a closed activation loop appears new; its evidence is currently one seed per arm. Second, the empirical observation that a small module at mid-depth of a frozen GDN hybrid restores exact needle recall under windowed attention while the same module cannot do so for a pure-attention model; whatever the mechanism, this is an interesting fact about hybrids, but its interpretation is unresolved. Third, the escalating, leaky-integral controller for multi-turn harm pressure and the fast-minus-slow "gist" direction for shortening reasoning are, as far as this search found, not published in this form; both are unrun, and their nearest neighbours (CAST, PID steering, dynamic activation composition, concise-CoT vectors) are close enough that the novelty will rest entirely on the experiments.

## Biological accuracy

The mapping is mostly defensible as metaphor, but several statements in the docstrings and design notes are inaccurate or one-sided. `ca2.py` says CA2 "lacks mossy-fibre input" and receives projections "no other CA field gets". Cell-type-specific tracing reported direct dentate input to CA2 ([Kohara et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3614)), supramammillary fibres also target the dentate gyrus, and CA2 is distinguished more by its strong entorhinal layer II drive and its powerful output to CA1 ([Chevaleyre & Siegelbaum 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.04.013); [Dudek et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2015.22)). The social-memory role of CA2 ([Hitti & Siegelbaum 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13028)) does not straightforwardly support a "norm" or alignment controller; the genesis notes already acknowledge that CA2 is better described as a context selector.

The time channel is described as Shankar–Howard time cells. What it implements, a bank of leaky integrators with log-spaced rates, is the Laplace-domain layer of that model ([Shankar & Howard 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00212)); time cells require the approximate inverse transform. The integrator layer itself has a better biological match in the slowly drifting temporal-context signals of lateral entorhinal cortex ([Tsao et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0459-6)), which would also put the clock in EC rather than in hippocampus proper.

The Treves–Rolls principle that the repository cites, dentate input for encoding and the direct perforant path for retrieval ([Treves & Rolls 1992](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450020209)), is honoured only in the temporal loop (`read="direct"`). In the Titans and GDN memories the read query is computed from the same DG code as the write key, which is the opposite arrangement; physiological evidence that CA3 completes degraded input while DG separates it ([Neunuebel & Knierim 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.017); [Leutgeb et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135801)) argues for the split. The T16 result (a DG read completes noisy cues better than a direct read at 4× expansion and 10% sparsity) is not evidence against Treves–Rolls, because this DG has no learned separation objective; random expansion plus sparsification decorrelates only in specific regimes ([Babadi & Sompolinsky 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.07.035)).

Heteroassociative successor storage in CA3 is well supported, from asymmetric plasticity models in the bibliography to the predictive-map account ([Stachenfeld et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650)). The attached [Samsonovich & Ascoli 2005](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.85205) goes further: CA3 stores a graph of transitions and retrieval is a search through that graph, a pathfinding process. The repository uses a single successor step. P4a, where the loop recovers one digit of four, is exactly the failure one-step lookup predicts, and chained successor retrieval is the experiment the attached paper suggests.

Adult human neurogenesis, which motivates the reserve units, is contested: one group reported it falls to undetectable levels in adults ([Sorrells et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature25975)) and another that it persists through ageing ([Boldrini et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2018.03.015)). The CA1 match/mismatch role behind the comparator has human imaging support ([Duncan et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20933)), and the CA1→CA3 inhibitory back-projection that the op-amp models is anatomically real ([Sik et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8085161)), though sparse; treating it as the stabilising element of a token-level loop is an engineering analogy, and the token-to-time mapping of the loop period $\tau$ has no physiological calibration.

## Proposed experiments

The order below is the order I would run them. Run times are extrapolated from the repository's own logs (474–677 s per 3,000-step needle run and 368–390 s per 1,000-step WikiText run on one RTX 5090; 13–23 minutes per 3,000-step CPU temporal-loop run).

**E1. MQAR re-benchmark of the memory modules (extends M1, M4–M7; H).** Hypothesis: with a proper protocol, standard GDN, deep Titans and full attention all bind, and the tied/DG addresses help only in sample efficiency or at loads near $d_k$. Design: the Zoology MQAR generator (vocabulary 8,192; sequence lengths 64–512; 4–64 pairs), with models at widths 64, 128 and 256: full attention, sliding window, repository GDN, the same GDN with random-initialised convolutions, GDN with tied and DG addresses, Titans deep and Titans-GDN, and Strategy I. Sweep four learning rates, three seeds each. Add two metrics the current task lacks: accuracy on the first query only, where elimination cannot help, and the binding-free baseline reported beside every number. Also run the factorial for M6 (tied q/k × linear/deep × per-token/chunk updates × heads). Cost: roughly 1,100 small runs at about 3 minutes each, about 55 GPU-hours, under 1.5 days on two GPUs.

**E2. Adapter controls and RULER for the pretrained needle (extends P1, P2, P4, W1; H).** Hypothesis: on Qwen3.5 a memory-free adapter of the same size restores recall, which would mean GDN carried the needle all along. Design: arms on Qwen3.5-2B and Qwen3-1.7B: frozen windowed; trained adapter with loop and clock off (same 4.38M parameters); LoRA rank 16 on the tap block; clock only; default loop; horizon loop; horizon loop with successor-bound keys and a GDN side store (W1). Three seeds, 6,000 steps (the horizon run was still improving at 3,000). Evaluate on RULER S-NIAH 1–3, MK-NIAH and MV-NIAH at 1k, 2k and 4k tokens, with UUID values to remove the digit prior, and log per-digit loss. Cost: 42 runs at about 20 minutes, about 15 GPU-hours.

**E3. Successor pathfinding (extends P4a, W1, M5; H).** Hypothesis: iterating the successor lookup $k$ times, as in the Samsonovich–Ascoli pathfinding model, recovers all digits of a multi-token value, where single-step lookup recovers one. Design: GDN side memory with successor keys on the all-windowed models of E2, comparing 1, 2 and 4 chained reads per generated token, plus a RULER multi-hop variable-tracking task. Metric: exact match and per-position accuracy. Cost: within E2's budget, about 5 extra GPU-hours.

**E4. Domain-adaptation control for perplexity (extends P5, O4; M).** Design: Qwen3 and Qwen3.5 windowed; parameter-matched LoRA trained on WikiText under the same window; side channels with memory ablated; default and horizon kernels; three seeds. Metric: perplexity on all tokens and on tokens whose best predictor lies more than $W$ tokens back (for example, second occurrences of rare n-grams), plus PG-19 or BABILong ([Kuratov et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10149)) for long-range reasoning. Cost: 30 runs at about 7 minutes, under 4 GPU-hours.

**E5. Multi-seed temporal-loop suite (extends T2, T3, T9, T11–T16, O2, S3; H).** Design: five seeds for each of the 14 configurations already scripted, a trained (not zero-shot) recency task with a curriculum on the separation, and bootstrap 95% intervals. Cost: about 70 CPU runs, 16 CPU-hours, about two hours on 16 cores.

**E6. DG pattern separation where it can matter (extends M7, T13, T16, W4, track 3; M).** Hypothesis: sparse DG addresses reduce interference only when keys are similar or loads exceed $d_k$. Design: MQAR with key similarity controlled (shared-prefix keys, or key embeddings with cosine $\rho\in\{0,0.5,0.8,0.95\}$) at loads $P/d_k\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}$; arms dense tied, learned DG k-WTA at 2%, 5% and 10%, fixed random expansion with k-WTA, product keys and top-k experts; DG used only on the write path versus on both paths (Treves–Rolls). Five seeds. Metric: recall against similarity and load. Cost: about 300 small runs, about 25 GPU-hours.

**E7. Replay controls (extends M3, O1, W3; M).** Design: ten sequential fact tasks with overlapping keys and buffers of 16, 64 and 256; arms naive, uniform replay, reservoir, DER++, EWC, novelty-prioritised replay, sleep only, awake only; five seeds; then the LoRA-consolidation version on a frozen model. Metric: average accuracy and backward transfer with intervals. Cost: CPU, a few hours.

**E8. Alignment arms on standard protocols (extends A1, A2, A6, J1; H for the alignment track).** Hypothesis: the graded controller reaches the static arm's harmful-refusal rate at lower over-refusal. Design: Qwen3-1.7B aligned, abliterated and base; arms none, static (CAA), CAST, PID ([Nguyen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04309)), graded and graded-self; directions and probes from three disjoint prompt splits; probe trained on one distribution and tested on another. Evaluate harmfulness with the HarmBench classifier ([Mazeika et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249)) and StrongREJECT ([Souly et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10260)), over-refusal on all 450 XSTest prompts ([Röttger et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263)) and OR-Bench ([Cui et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20947)), with WildGuard ([Han et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18495)) or Llama Guard ([Inan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06674)) as a second judge, and capability on MMLU, GSM8K and perplexity. Compare arms at matched over-refusal (a selectivity curve over gains), not at one gain. Cost: about 2,000 prompts × 6 arms × 3 models × 256 tokens, under 10 GPU-hours.

**E9. Gist steering of reasoning (extends R2–R4; M).** Design: Qwen3 thinking checkpoints (1.7B and 4B) on GSM8K ([Cobbe et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)), MATH-500 ([Hendrycks et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874)) and AIME 2024–25 (eight samples per problem); arms full thinking, no-think, budget forcing at matched token budgets, SEAL, concise vector, and gist at three gains after the S1 saturation sweep. Metric: accuracy against reasoning tokens (Pareto front), with paired bootstrap. Cost: about 9M generated tokens per arm, 1–2 days on two GPUs.

**E10. Skip-connection track at small LM scale (track 1; M).** Hypothesis: a temporoammonic skip (embedding stream into a late comparator) adds nothing over value residuals, DenseFormer or hyper-connections at matched parameters. Design: 125M-parameter models on 2.5B tokens, five arms, three seeds, evaluated on perplexity, MQAR and RULER. Cost: about 15 runs of 5 hours, about 75 GPU-hours.

## Open questions

The central open question for track 4 is what the GDN layers of a frozen hybrid actually retain, and at what load they stop retaining it; E2 with a memory-free adapter answers whether the Qwen3.5 result is memory or repair, and multi-key RULER variants answer how far the fixed state goes. For the temporal loop, the question is whether a closed, regulated loop offers anything that an extra attention head with an age bias does not; the loop is $O(T)$, so it does not shrink the KV cache, and if it is to support never-ending chats the fixed-state GDN version (O3) has to be built and compared with it. For tracks 2 and 3 the questions are empirical and unanswered: whether a leaky-integral controller is more selective than CAST or a PID controller at matched over-refusal, whether a gist direction shortens reasoning without the accuracy loss of budget truncation, and whether DG sparsity buys anything on keys that are actually similar. A last question is methodological. Several register entries were marked Supported on one seed and later revised; adopting a rule that Supported requires three seeds and an interval that excludes the baseline would prevent the next round of corrections.
