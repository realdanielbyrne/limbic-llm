# CA2 as a context side channel: steering for alignment and reasoning

## Scope

This review covers the CA2 track of limbic-llm (commit 3799b36): the FiLM modulator in `limbic_llm/ca2.py`, the alignment controller in `experiments/alignment/` (`steering.py`, `probe.py`, `run.py`, `judge.py`, `data.py`), and the proposals in `results/evolve/NOTEBOOK.md` and `REGISTER.md` (S1–S8, A1–A7, R1–R5, J1–J5). Two claims receive the most scrutiny. The first is the graded escalating controller: a leaky integral of a probe's harm score drives a tanh-capped gain on the refusal direction, ending in "Asimov" degeneration past the cap. The second is gist momentum steering, which injects the normalised difference between a fast and a slow running average of the residual stream to shorten chain-of-thought. In short, both mechanisms are small, defensible recombinations of published components. Neither has a direct published twin among the roughly eighty sources checked here. The CA2 framing adds little beyond a name, and some of the biology the code cites is out of date. No experiment in this track has run on a real model yet, so every empirical claim below is about prior work, not about limbic-llm results.

## Closest prior work

### CA2 biology

The best-supported function of CA2 is social recognition memory, not norm enforcement. Genetic silencing of CA2 pyramidal output abolishes social memory while sparing spatial and object memory [Hitti & Siegelbaum 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13028). Excitotoxic CA2 lesions give the same result [Stevenson & Caldwell 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12689). Dorsal CA2 is needed for encoding, consolidation and recall of social memory, which it supports through a projection onto the ventral CA1 subfield that holds social engrams [Meira et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06501-w) [Okuyama et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf7003). CA2 ensembles carry social-novelty signals, and these are disrupted in a 22q11.2 deletion model [Donegan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-00720-5). The neuromodulation is real and specific. Vasopressin 1b receptors in CA2 control aggression and CA2 plasticity [Pagani et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2014.47), and targeted activation of vasopressin input to CA2 strongly enhances social memory [Smith et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/mp.2015.189). Oxytocin switches CA2 pyramidal cells into a bursting mode [Tirko et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.09.008), and oxytocin receptors in anterior CA2/CA3 are needed to discriminate social stimuli [Raam et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-02173-0). The supramammillary nucleus sends a social-novelty signal to CA2 and a contextual-novelty signal to the dentate gyrus [Chen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2771-1). Through the lateral septum, CA2 *disinhibits* social aggression [Leroy et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0772-0). If CA2 regulates behaviour, then, it regulates it toward a particular conspecific, in either direction.

That evidence makes the repo's founding analogy, "societal norms drive CA2", indefensible as stated. No study cited here, or found in this search, shows CA2 encoding norms, rules or the appropriateness of an action. CA2 encodes *who* (identity, familiarity, novelty), and downstream circuits decide *what to do* about it. The notebook already concedes this and recasts CA2 as a "context selector for social behaviour". That version is defensible, and it maps better onto README idea "CA2 as identity steering" (per-speaker vectors) than onto a harm controller. A geometric account of how hippocampal representations meet social-memory demands is already in the repo bibliography (`boyle2024tuned`).

CA2's temporal signature runs on hours, not tokens. CA2 population codes drift over hours to days in an unchanged environment; after six hours the drift already exceeds the difference between two distinct enclosures [Mankin et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.001). Social and novel stimuli trigger global remapping of CA2 place fields without changes in firing rate [Alexander et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms10300). This is an episode tag, a slowly changing label that tells episodes apart. It is not a moving average of the input. A drifting temporal-context signal integrated from experience has also been shown in lateral entorhinal cortex [Tsao et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0459-6), so a temporal-context model ([Howard & Kahana 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmps.2001.1388)) is no more a CA2 property than an EC one.

CA2's role in sharp-wave ripples is real but contested in sign. In one study, superficial CA2 cells fired before CA3 and CA1 during ripples, and deep "ramping" CA2 cells built up activity beforehand, which the authors read as CA2 triggering ripples [Oliva et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.08.008). Another found CA2 "N cells" that fall silent during ripples and encode position during immobility and sleep [Kay et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17144). In a third, chemogenetically *increasing* CA2 activity raised low-gamma power and *reduced* ripple occurrence [Alexander et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.38052), and chronically silencing CA2 transmission produced hippocampal hyperexcitability [Boehringer et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.04.014). CA2 ripples reactivate social-memory ensembles, and silencing them during sleep impairs social memory [Oliva et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2758-y). Taken together, CA2 behaves more like a gate and stabiliser of hippocampal excitability, with its own replay channel for social content, than like a gain booster. That reading favours the repo's stability programme (S1–S8) over the "amplifier" reading of CA2. Anatomically, CA2 receives unusually strong entorhinal layer II input [Chevaleyre & Siegelbaum 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.04.013), has a place-map topography that grades from CA3 [Lu et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.07.007), and resists the usual LTP [Dudek et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2015.22).

### Steering and refusal geometry

Every primitive in the controller is published. Adding a contrastive activation difference to the residual stream is ActAdd [Turner et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248). The mean-of-differences version applied at all post-prompt positions is CAA [Panickssery et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681). RepE generalised both into reading and controlling population-level concepts [Zou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405), and head-restricted shifts with a tunable strength go back to ITI [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03341). Subtracting the mean over all activations from the target mean [Jorgensen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03813) is the static, dataset-level ancestor of the gist's fast-minus-slow contrast. A single difference-of-means direction mediates refusal across 13 chat models: ablating it disables refusal, and adding it induces refusal even on harmless prompts [Arditi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717). `probe.diff_of_means` implements exactly this.

The single-direction picture has since been refined, with direct consequences for A6. Refusal is better described by several representationally independent directions and multi-dimensional concept cones [Wollschläger et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420). Steering along any refusal-related direction traces nearly the same refusal–over-refusal trade-off curve; the direction changes *how* the model refuses, not *whether* [Joad et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02132) (EMNLP 2026). LLMs also encode harmfulness separately from refusal. Jailbreaks and adversarial fine-tuning weaken the refusal signal but leave the harmfulness judgement largely intact, and a "Latent Guard" read from that representation matches Llama Guard 3 8B [J. Zhao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11878). Together these predict that the *direction* matters little in A2/A6. Selectivity will come almost entirely from the gate, the harm probe and its timing. That makes A1/J1 the real experiment.

The critiques of steering reliability apply in full. Steerability varies widely across inputs, and some concepts fail out of distribution [Tan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12404). Unreliability is predicted by how poorly a linear direction approximates the behaviour [Braun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17881) (a thesis-length preprint). On AxBench, prompting beats every representation-steering method, and SAE steering is not competitive [Wu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148). Amplifying refusal-mediating SAE features improves single- and multi-turn jailbreak robustness but degrades benchmarks even on safe inputs [O'Brien et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11296). Latent-space monitors, including harmfulness probes, can be evaded by obfuscated activations, with recall falling from 100% to 0% at a 90% jailbreak rate [Bailey et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09565). This bears directly on A5.

### Adaptive, conditional and feedback steering

The space between "static" and "graded" is already crowded. CAST gates a refusal vector on a condition vector computed from the prompt [Lee et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907). AdaSteer sets prompt-dependent coefficients along a rejection direction and a harmfulness direction by logistic regression on the last prompt-token residual [W. Zhao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09466). That is a probe-to-gain map, without temporal integration. SafeSwitch uses a prober on internal states to switch in a tuned safety head only when needed [Han et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01042). Jailbreak Antidote shifts a sparse 5% of the hidden state along a safety direction with an adjustable strength at runtime [Shen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02298). Two 2026 projection-aware methods (StTP, StMP) steer only the *tokens* whose activations fall below a logistic decision boundary. They use a direction taken from the aligned model to restore alignment under malicious system prompts, avoid the repetition build-up that uniform steering causes over multi-turn conversations, and restore honesty in an emergently misaligned model [Herbster et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08169). For A6 this is the closest precedent: aligned-sibling direction, a probe gate, token-level selectivity, and a check of capability and coherence.

Dynamic per-step intensity is also published. Dynamic Activation Composition sets the steering strength at each generation step from the KL shift that steering induces, and finds strong steering is mostly needed on the first few tokens [Scalena et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17563). Spherical Steering replaces addition with norm-preserving geodesic rotation plus a confidence gate [You et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08169), which is S7 already done on multiple-choice benchmarks. The control-theoretic framing belongs to PID Steering, which casts existing steering as proportional control and adds integral and derivative terms. Its integral, however, accumulates error *across layers*, not across tokens [Nguyen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04309) (ICLR 2026). The token-chunk PID controller for reasoning that the notebook cites as "STU-PID" [Bharadwaj 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18831) has been **withdrawn by its author** in favour of the PID Steering paper. The current version reports 85.7→89.6% on a GSM8K subset with 1026→790 tokens, not the figures in the notebook.

Temporal accumulation of harm signals exists on the *monitoring* side. The streaming content monitor scores harmfulness per token on partial outputs and stops generation once k tokens exceed a threshold, a cumulative count [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09996) (NeurIPS 2025). Hidden-state token probes do the same inside the decode loop and note that the probe's weight vector doubles as a steering direction [Shu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10487) (technical report). For multi-turn attacks, THRD aggregates turn-level risk with attenuation and trend terms [Ma et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01738). A neural barrier function treats the dialogue as a state-space system and filters queries that would push it into an unsafe set [Hu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00187) (TMLR). The threat these address is real. Crescendo escalates from benign turns [Russinovich et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01833), and at the representation level Crescendo responses move *toward the benign region* as turns accumulate, which explains why circuit breakers fail on it [Bullwinkel et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02956). Circuit breakers themselves fine-tune harmful representations toward incoherent or refusal states [Zou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04313). That is the learned-in-weights version of Asimov's freeze, as the notebook says. Adversarial training in latent space [Casper et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05030) and refusal-feature adversarial training [Yu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20089) are the training-time alternatives. Llama Guard [Inan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06674), HarmBench ([Mazeika et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249)), XSTest ([Röttger et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263)) and OR-Bench [Cui et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20947) supply the guard and evaluation baselines.

### Re-aligning abliterated models

A6 is partly anticipated. Adding the refusal direction back is known to induce refusal [Arditi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717), so the static arm is close to a tautology, as the notebook admits. The defensive literature comes at abliteration from the other side. Extended-refusal fine-tuning spreads the refusal signal so abliteration removes at most about 10% of refusals [Abu Shairah et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19056) (preprint). Refusal aliases obscure the direction so it is harder to extract [Truong 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18093) (preprint, 2026). Same-lineage comparisons of aligned and abliterated Qwen and Gemma models are starting to appear [Li et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05842). As far as this search found, nobody has published a *runtime* hook that restores refusal selectively in an abliterated model, uses that model's own surviving harmfulness representation as the gate, and compares it against the never-aligned base. That combination is a legitimate, if incremental, contribution, and the base-model arm (H3) is the most informative part.

### Steering chain-of-thought

Fixed-vector CoT compression is mature, and the repo's survey of it is accurate. SEAL suppresses reflection and transition thoughts [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07986). Activation-steered compression cuts CoT length by up to 67% with a KL-bounded strength [Azizi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04742). Manifold steering shows that a single overthinking direction plateaus and then hurts as strength rises, because high-dimensional noise accumulates [Huang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22411). Thinking-speed vectors with real-time difficulty estimation give +1.3% accuracy at −8.6% tokens [Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03704) (NeurIPS 2025). CoT-Valve does the same in parameter space [Ma et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09601). ThinkEdit is the counterweight: overly *short* reasoning is itself a failure mode governed by a linear direction [Sun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22048). Backtracking and uncertainty are linear, steerable behaviours [Venhoff et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18167). Memory-retrieved and routed steering are published as well. STIR builds a library of state-conditioned correction vectors from the model's own rollouts [Shi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04925) (EMNLP 2026), RISER routes among reasoning-skill vectors [Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09269), and SOPHIA indexes steering vectors by latent-state *transitions* and breaks self-loops online [Yu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18100) (preprint). SOPHIA reports that failed reasoning traces get stuck in self-loops. That finding is the strongest prior evidence against an open-loop gist that reinforces the current thought.

Continuous and recurrent reasoning is the other neighbour. Coconut feeds the last hidden state back as the next input embedding [Hao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769). Soft Thinking feeds probability-weighted embedding mixtures back, with up to 22.4% fewer tokens [Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15778). Huginn iterates a recurrent block with the input re-injected at each step and scales test-time depth [Geiping et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171). Ouro pre-trains looped models with learned exit depth [Zhu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741). None of these is a *side channel*: all of them recur through the main pathway. The genesis note's mapping of Ouro to big-loop recurrence rather than CA2 is therefore correct, and Huginn belongs there too. Finally, "momentum" over activations has been published *across layers* rather than across time. STAR keeps an exponential average of late-layer residual updates in vision-language-action robot policies [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41009) (preprint), and DAMO (ICLR 2025) does the same for vision-language hallucination; DAMO is not cited here because no DOI or arXiv record could be verified.

## Novelty and anticipation

### Graded escalating controller

The controller is a recombination, and the novelty is narrow. Probe-gated steering (CAST, SafeSwitch, AdaSteer, StTP/StMP), per-step intensity (Dynamic Activation Composition), feedback-control framing (PID Steering), token-level harm accumulation (the streaming monitor's delay-k rule), and multi-turn risk accumulation with attenuation (THRD) are all published. No paper found here feeds a leaky integral of a residual-stream harm probe through a saturating gain onto the refusal direction, token by token, as a runtime hook. That specific pipeline is therefore unpublished, but the step from AdaSteer plus SCM to it is small. A reviewer would read it as a design variant, not a new mechanism.

The control-theoretic claims also need trimming. As implemented, the probe scores the *pre-injection* stream at the same layer, so within a forward pass the controller never sees its own output. The loop closes only through the sampled token, which is discrete, delayed and nonlinear. The op-amp and characteristic-polynomial analysis (K2–K3) therefore does not carry over to A4. Ringing would have to be measured as an alternation between refusal and compliance tokens, not predicted. The integrator also has no setpoint, so it is a first-order low-pass filter of the score scaled by 1/(1−λ), not integral control. At the defaults (λ = 0.9, κ = 0.5), sustained s ≈ 1 drives the gain to 0.46, 0.74, 0.875 and 0.938 of the cap after one to four tokens (0.967 after five). The time constant is about 10 tokens, and a prefill spike decays to 6% of its effect within 20 tokens. The controller is thus a soft switch with a three-token rise time, not an escalation over a conversation. Combined with `reset()` between sequences, multi-turn escalation and recovery (A3/H4) cannot occur at the defaults. It would need λ ≈ 0.998 (a 500-token time constant) or a separate turn-level integrator.

The "Asimov" endpoint is not new either. Circuit breakers deliberately route harmful representations toward incoherence, and over-strong steering is widely reported to degrade fluency. Treating degeneration past the cap as a designed behaviour, rather than a failure, is a framing choice. It is best reported honestly as a capability cost.

### Gist momentum steering

The gist direction is the most original piece in the track. No paper found here takes the steering direction from the normalised difference between a fast and a slow exponential average of the model's *own* residual stream over *time*. The nearest relatives are static mean-centring, cross-layer momentum in vision-language and vision-language-action models (STAR, DAMO), dynamic input-derived vectors (SADI [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12299)), and state-recurrent reasoning (Coconut, Huginn). The construction is also easy to describe in signal-processing terms. A fast-minus-slow EMA is a temporal band-pass filter (β = 0.9 and β_s = 0.995 pass structure lasting roughly 10–200 tokens), and injecting it with gain proportional to ‖h‖ is positive feedback on whatever the stream has been doing over that band. Prior work points to two ways this fails. Self-reinforcing loops are the main failure of reasoning traces (SOPHIA), and boosting a direction past a plateau injects noise (manifold steering). There is also a confound that the code does not handle. Residual streams carry a few massive, nearly input-independent activations [Sun et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762) (COLM 2024). They sit at a few token positions, such as the first token and delimiters. Each newline therefore puts a large transient into the fast average that the slow one does not cancel, and normalisation lets those spikes set the gist direction. This is a predicted confound, not a measured one, and E5 tests it. The notebook's "negative feedback" version (R3, J4) exists only as a plan. What is built is open-loop and capped. A claim of "gist momentum under negative feedback" therefore has no implementation behind it yet, and once built it would face PID Steering as the obvious baseline.

### Remaining CA2 components

The FiLM `CA2Modulator` is FiLM ([Perez et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11671)) conditioned on a per-channel causal exponential average. That average is a diagonal linear recurrence of the kind used in retention and state-space layers, so the module is anticipated, and the biology attached to it adds nothing. The fixed-vector CoT arms (R1, R2's baselines) re-describe SEAL, ASC and thinking-speed work. Memory-retrieved gists (R5) are anticipated by STIR, DSEM (`do2025dynamic`, in the repo bibliography) and especially SOPHIA. SOPHIA's state-transition-indexed bank is close to the repo's CA3 successor-transition store. The genuinely hippocampal contribution open to R5 would be retrieval keyed on CA3 successor transitions with CA1 novelty gating.

## Biological accuracy issues

Five statements in the repo should change. First, the `ca2.py` docstring says CA2 "lacks mossy-fibre input". Cell-type-specific tracing showed that dentate granule cells do project to CA2 [Kohara et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3614), so the classical "no mossy fibre" view is outdated. Second, the same docstring says CA2 receives "supramammillary projections that no other CA field gets". This is technically true of CA fields, but supramammillary input also targets the dentate gyrus, carrying a different novelty signal [Chen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2771-1). Third, the docstring presents a Howard–Kahana temporal context as a CA2 property. CA2's drift runs on hours to days [Mankin et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.001), the module's ρ = 0.9 gives about a 10-token memory, and integrated temporal context has also been recorded in lateral entorhinal cortex [Tsao et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0459-6). Fourth, the README places CA2 serially in "EC → DG → CA3 → CA2 → CA1". CA2 is better described as a parallel route that receives direct EC II input and projects to CA1, beside the CA3 → CA1 Schaffer pathway [Chevaleyre & Siegelbaum 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.04.013) [Dudek et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2015.22). Fifth, "social context and norms shape CA2" (NOTEBOOK, alignment proposal) should be replaced by the identity and social-novelty account. The repo already half-corrects this. If the project wants a biologically faithful CA2 experiment, the candidates are episode tagging by slow drift and identity-keyed steering, not harm control. CA2's documented role in gating ripples and excitability can still motivate the stability programme.

## Open questions

Several questions remain open. The first is whether any token-time integration adds selectivity once the prompt-level decision is good, or whether the leaky integrator simply re-implements CAST with smoothing. E2 answers this. The second is whether the harmfulness representation survives *generated* refusal text and multi-turn drift. The separation result was measured on instructions, and Crescendo appears to move representations toward benign. The third is whether norm-preserving rotation moves the saturation knee for refusal steering as it does on multiple-choice tasks (S7). The fourth is whether the gist can be made useful at all without a calibrated "on track" signal. Without one it amplifies whatever the model is already doing, including its errors. The fifth is whether a defensible CA2 analogue for LLMs is identity- or speaker-keyed context rather than harm control. That analogue would follow the social-identity literature, and README's "CA2 as identity steering" idea is the place to test it. The last is deployment. As the notebook notes, a runtime hook on open weights can be removed, so this track contributes measurement and closed-model guardrails, not tamper resistance. The abliteration defences that modify weights address a different threat model.
