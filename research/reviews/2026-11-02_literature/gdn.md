# Gated DeltaNet as a CA3-like memory in limbic-llm: a literature review of track 4

## Scope

Most of what limbic-llm says about Gated DeltaNet (GDN) is already in the fast-weight and test-time-regression literature. That includes the reading of the GDN update as one decayed gradient step on an associative loss, the claim that one-hot keys make the state exact RAM, and successor-bound addressing. The repository adds two things. The first is a reproducible bridge into a production hybrid model, Qwen3.5. The second is an empirical observation: a small module trained on the residual stream lets the frozen GDN layers of Qwen3.5 recall a needle that the frozen model cannot recall. That observation is interesting, but its current interpretation is under-controlled. The small-model capacity results that support the "CA3 = exact RAM" story (M4–M6) also do not test the claims they are cited for.

This review covers the fourth of the user's research tracks: Gated DeltaNet and other linear-attention layers as a soft, RNN-style autoregressive memory (the CA3 analogue), Titans-style test-time memory, memory capacity, and consolidation. It is based on repository commit `3799b36`. The code read for it is `limbic_llm/gated_delta.py`, `neural_memory.py`, `titans_hybrid.py`, `replay.py`, `temporal_loop.py` (the `TimeChannel` and `HippocampalLoop` paths) and `experiments/pretrained_loop.py`. The results read are `results/gdn_capacity.json`, `assoc_recall*.json`, `results/pretrained_HANDOFF.md` and `results/evolve/REGISTER.md`. The register items assessed are M4–M6, P1–P6, W1, W3, O3 and O5. The CA2 steering, DG-as-MoE and skip-connection tracks are covered only where they touch the GDN memory.

## Closest prior work

### Fast weights, linear attention and the delta rule

All of the repository's GDN formalism comes from one line of work, now about thirty years old, which treats a matrix-valued recurrent state as a set of fast weights programmed by a slow network. [Schmidhuber 1992](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1992.4.1.131) introduced a slow network that writes outer-product updates into a fast-weight matrix. [Ba et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06258) revived the idea as a decaying Hebbian memory of the recent past. [Katharopoulos et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16236) showed that kernelised attention is an RNN with an outer-product state. [Schlag et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174) (already in the repository bibliography) made the link explicit and derived two results the repository relies on. A purely additive linear-attention state can hold at most d_k orthogonal associations. Replacing the additive write with the Widrow–Hoff delta rule lets the memory overwrite an association instead of superposing it. [Yang et al. 2024a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484) made DeltaNet trainable at scale with a WY/UT chunkwise algorithm, and [Yang et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464) added the scalar decay α_t from Mamba-2 to obtain Gated DeltaNet. `gated_delta_chunked` is a careful, independent reimplementation of that algorithm, a unit-lower-triangular solve within each chunk, and it is correct as far as this review checked. As a re-derivation of a published kernel, however, it is not a contribution.

The central interpretive claim in the `gated_delta.py` docstring is that the GDN update equals one SGD step of size β_t on the Titans loss ½‖Mk − v‖², taken at the decayed state. That claim is now textbook. The GDN paper itself presents the delta rule as online regression. [Liu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14207) (Longhorn) derive state-space layers as amortised solutions of exactly this online-learning problem. [Sun et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620) (TTT layers) generalise it to an inner model trained by self-supervised gradient steps. [Behrouz et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663) (Titans, in the bibliography) add momentum and a deep memory. [Behrouz et al. 2025a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13173) (Miras) and [Wang, Shi & Fox 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352) each give a taxonomy in which GDN, Mamba-2, Longhorn, TTT and Titans differ only in the attentional bias (loss), the retention regulariser (decay) and the optimiser. [von Oswald et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07677) and [von Oswald et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05858) had already shown that linear self-attention can implement a gradient step, or an optimal least-squares "mesa" solve, on in-context regression. The repository should cite Miras and Wang et al. for the equivalence rather than present it as an observation.

### Successors to GDN, and where the state lives in 2025–2026 models

The design space around GDN has moved quickly, and several recent variants bear directly on what the repository's GDN modules can and cannot do. Mamba and Mamba-2 ([Gu & Dao 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752); [Dao & Gu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060)) are the decay-only special case. RWKV-7 ([Peng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456)) uses a generalised delta rule with a vector-valued in-context learning rate. DeltaProduct ([Siems et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10297)) takes several Householder steps per token to improve state tracking. Kimi Delta Attention ([Kimi Team 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692)) adds channel-wise decay and ships in a 3:1 KDA:MLA hybrid. Gated DeltaNet-2 ([Hatamizadeh, Choi & Kautz 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22791), preprint) separates the erase gate from the write gate and reports its largest gains on RULER multi-key needle retrieval. That separation matters for the repository's "RAM" framing: an exact slot overwrite needs erase = write = 1 on one slot and 0 elsewhere, which scalar-β GDN cannot express. [Behrouz et al. 2025b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735) (ATLAS) optimise the memory over a sliding window of past tokens (the Omega rule) rather than one token. [Tandon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675) (TTT-E2E, preprint) report that a sliding-window Transformer with meta-learned test-time training scales with context like full attention, while Mamba-2 and GDN do not. [Guo et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04761) (log-linear attention) let the state grow logarithmically, between a fixed-size state and a full cache. The hybrid ratio the repository inherits is also well studied. Jamba ([Lieber et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887)), Hymba ([Dong et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13676)) and the NVIDIA Mamba-2-Hybrid study ([Waleffe et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07887)) established that a small fraction of attention layers recovers the recall that pure recurrent models lose. Qwen3.5 (in the repository bibliography as `qwen2026qwen35`) uses three GDN layers per gated-attention layer. A design report for a later Qwen model keeps one full-attention layer in every four ([Qiu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320), preprint).

### Recall, capacity and the limits of a fixed state

The theory that decides whether a fixed-size state can be "exact RAM" is settled at the level the repository needs. A linear correlation-matrix memory with orthonormal keys retrieves exactly up to d_k items and degrades with crosstalk beyond that ([Kohonen 1972](https://doi.org/10.1109/TC.1972.5008975)). Non-linear, thresholded readouts store more. These include the binary Willshaw net ([Willshaw, Buneman & Longuet-Higgins 1969](https://doi.org/10.1038/222960a0)), the Hopfield network with capacity about 0.138N ([Hopfield 1982](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.79.8.2554); [Amit, Gutfreund & Sompolinsky 1985](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.55.1530)), sparse low-activity networks whose capacity grows as roughly 1/(a·ln(1/a)) at coding level a ([Tsodyks & Feigel'man 1988](https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/6/2/002)), and dense or modern Hopfield networks with polynomial to exponential capacity (Krotov & Hopfield 2016 and Ramsauer et al. 2021, both in the bibliography). This bears directly on the dentate-gyrus (DG) story. Sparsifying the keys raises capacity only when the readout is non-linear. GDN's readout o = Sq is linear, so with k-WTA keys it is still bounded by d_k exact associations per head.

On the empirical side, the Zoology programme defined multi-query associative recall (MQAR). It showed that most of the perplexity gap between gated-convolution models and attention is a recall gap ([Arora et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927)). Based then showed a Pareto frontier between recall and recurrent-state size ([Arora et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668)). [Jelassi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032) proved that a fixed-state model cannot copy strings whose information exceeds its state. [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07145) found that the passkey length a model can retrieve scales with its state size, and that models trained on contexts short for their state never learn to forget. [Okpekpe & Orvieto 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19029) showed that recurrent models' AR results are unusually sensitive to the learning rate, enough to distort published comparisons. That caveat applies directly to M4. A separate limit applies to state tracking. Diagonal and positive-eigenvalue linear RNNs cannot track state beyond TC⁰ ([Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819)), and allowing negative eigenvalues (β ∈ (0, 2)) fixes parity ([Grazzi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537)). The repository's `_gates` uses β = σ(·) ∈ (0, 1), so every GDN memory in limbic-llm inherits that limitation.

### Where retrieval lives in hybrids

Recent interpretability and architecture-search work agrees that retrieval in trained hybrids sits in a few attention heads, not in the recurrent state. That is the most important context for P1 and P2. In both Transformers and SSMs, in-context retrieval runs through a small number of Gather-and-Aggregate heads ([Bick, Xing & Gu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18574)), the hybrid analogue of the retrieval heads found in Transformers ([Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15574)). [Wang et al. 2025a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06457) found that recall in hybrids improves sharply with more full-attention layers, especially at ratios denser than 3:1. They also found that the best standalone linear layer (GDN) is not the best hybrid partner. Jet-Nemotron's PostNAS search ([Gu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15884)) found that only a few attention layers matter for retrieval tasks. Most directly, [Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05842) (HLA, preprint) measured pretrained Qwen3.5 GDN layers. They report that a needle chunk's contribution to the state falls by several orders of magnitude as later 512-token chunks are processed, and that query-dependent chunk routing gives up to 3.97 points on RULER for Qwen3.5 models of 0.8B–9B parameters. [Cabannes et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24552) (SWAX) supply the training-dynamics explanation: when local attention can solve retrieval, the recurrent layer never learns long-term memorisation, and shortening the window forces it to. A frozen Qwen3.5 whose GDN layers cannot recall a 500-token needle once attention is windowed (P1) is therefore the expected result, not a new one. For the companion KV-trimming project, LoLA ([McDermott, Heath & Parhi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23666)) and learnable token eviction ([He & Garner 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20787)) already implement a three-tier memory. Recent tokens sit in a window, pairs that are hard to memorise sit in a sparse exact cache, and everything else is folded into the linear-attention state. LoLA chooses what goes into the exact cache by the state's own self-recall error, which is exactly the `err` that `gated_delta_scan` already returns.

### Retrofitting memory into pretrained models

Adding a delta-rule memory to a pretrained Transformer, frozen or lightly tuned, has been done several times, and the most recent instance anticipates W1 closely. Infini-attention ([Munkhdalai, Faruqui & Gopal 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07143)) adds a compressive linear memory with a delta-rule variant to every attention layer and continues pre-training. The Recurrent Memory Transformer and its associative variant ([Bulatov, Kuratov & Burtsev 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06881); [Rodkin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04841)) carry a delta-rule associative memory across segments and answer single-fact questions over very long inputs; ARMT reports 79.9% at 50M tokens on BABILong. These descend from memory tokens ([Burtsev et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11527)). Linearisation work distils pretrained attention into linear or Mamba layers ([Zhang et al. 2024a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10254); [Wang et al. 2024a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15237); [Ashrafi Fashi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24715), preprint). Most relevant is δ-mem ([Lei et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12357), preprint). It attaches an 8×8 delta-rule state to a frozen full-attention backbone, a Qwen3-4B checkpoint in its released adapter. The state's readout drives low-rank corrections to attention, and δ-mem reports gains on memory-heavy agent benchmarks. W1, a GDN side memory on frozen Qwen3-1.7B, is the same design family. It should be positioned against δ-mem and use it as a baseline.

### Steering and injecting into recurrent state

Steering recurrent models through their state is established, and state-level adaptation beats prompt-level adaptation for SSMs. [Paulo, Marshall & Belrose 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05971) showed that contrastive activation addition transfers to Mamba and RWKV, and that steering through the compressed state can improve it. [Galim et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09016) found that LoRA on the linear projections beats other parameter-efficient methods on SSMs but fails on the SSM modules themselves. [Kang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03499) found that prompt- and prefix-based methods ([Li & Liang 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)) perform poorly on SSMs. They proposed state-offset tuning instead: a learned offset added to the state at every step, which outperforms them. For reading the state, the hidden-attention reformulation of Mamba ([Ali, Zimerman & Wolf 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01590)) and autoencoder probes of Mamba states ([Hossain et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15653), preprint) give the attribution and probing tools. The probe study reports that Mamba states retain numbers especially poorly, which is directly relevant to a digit needle. Linear probes in the sense of [Alain & Bengio 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01644) are the cheapest first step. The repository's residual-stream injection at one block is the transformer-style choice. For a hybrid, writing into the GDN state itself is the native alternative, and it has not yet been tried here.

### Sequence memory and successor-bound keys

Binding each cue to the item that follows it is an old idea with recent machine-learning versions. Asymmetric Hebbian couplings that map pattern t onto pattern t+1 give a Hopfield network sequence recall ([Sompolinsky & Kanter 1986](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.2861)). [Chaudhry et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04532) extend this to long sequences with dense non-linearities. The hippocampal versions are Levy (1996) and Jensen & Lisman (1996), both in the bibliography, together with the attached [Samsonovich & Ascoli 2005](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.85205). That model treats CA3 as a store of transitions between successive states, so that retrieval can follow a path through the stored graph. In sequence models the same binding shows up in three places. H3's shift-SSM ([Fu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14052)) was built to give associative recall a token shift. Induction heads ([Olsson et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895)) compose a previous-token head with a content match. The short convolution in GDN, which the repository's docstring credits for "the token shift that binds a key to the value that follows it", serves the same purpose. Memory Mosaics ([Zhang et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06394)) build entire language models from associative memories whose keys summarise the past and whose values summarise the next token. That is successor-bound addressing as an architectural principle. Neural Turing Machines ([Graves, Wayne & Danihelka 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5401)) are the canonical differentiable RAM. The repository's `"tied"` mode, a single address a_t used as the query at t and as the write key for v_{t+1}, hard-wires the induction-head shift into the GDN key. That is a clean engineering simplification of known mechanisms, not a new one.

### Biology-flavoured key–value memory

Several groups have argued that biological memory is a key–value store and that attention and fast weights are hippocampal. [Whittington, Warren & Behrens 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04035) (in the bibliography) relate Transformers to the Tolman–Eichenbaum Machine and to hippocampal place and grid cells. [Tyulmankov et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13976) derive biologically plausible plasticity rules for key–value memories. [Gershman, Fiete & Irie 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02950) review the case that the brain separates keys, optimised for discriminability in retrieval, from values, optimised for fidelity in storage. That split cuts against a reading in which GDN's whole matrix state corresponds to one hippocampal subfield.

### Consolidation and continual learning

The complementary-learning-systems (CLS) component (`replay.py`, W3) sits in a crowded field. The 2026 literature already contains several "LLM sleep" systems. The theory is McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly (1995), in the bibliography, as updated by [Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.05.004). Brain-inspired generative replay ([van de Ven, Siegelmann & Tolias 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17866-2)) and sleep-like unsupervised replay ([Tadros et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34938-7)) cover the classical network side. Logit-anchored replay is dark experience replay (Buzzega et al. 2020, in the bibliography), and priority sampling is prioritised experience replay ([Schaul et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)). For LLMs specifically, several methods consolidate knowledge into parameters while limiting interference:

- MemoryLLM and M+ build self-updatable latent memory pools ([Wang et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04624); [Wang et al. 2025b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00592)).
- O-LoRA restricts updates to orthogonal subspaces ([Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14152)).
- Self-synthesised rehearsal replays model-generated data ([Huang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01244)).
- Sparse memory finetuning updates only the memory-layer slots a new fact activates ([Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103)). It reports an 11% drop in NaturalQuestions F1, against 89% for full finetuning and 71% for LoRA.
- Self-distillation fine-tuning uses the model's own on-policy outputs ([Shenfeld et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897), preprint).

Two 2026 preprints use the sleep metaphor directly. [Lee et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099) run offline recurrent passes that turn recent context into SSM fast weights before the KV cache is cleared. That is close to what a never-ending chat with a GDN memory needs. [Behrouz et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979) distil short-term memories into a larger network and add a "dreaming" self-improvement phase. It builds on Nested Learning's continuum memory system ([Behrouz et al. 2025c](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695)). The repository's `orthogonal_projection` hook, its choice of slow parameters and its novelty-weighted priorities are each found in at least one of these.

## Where limbic-llm is novel and where it is anticipated

### Anticipated: the formal identities

Three statements the repository presents as insights are published results. "The GDN update is one SGD step on the Titans loss at the decayed state" is in the GDN paper and is formalised in Longhorn, Miras and test-time regression. "One-hot keys with α = β = 1 make the state exact RAM" follows directly from Kohonen's correlation-matrix memory and Schlag et al.'s capacity analysis. The repository credits only the Qwenputer blog, which is not peer-reviewed and should not be the sole citation. "Successor-bound keys a_{t−1} ↦ v_t give sequence memory" is Sompolinsky–Kanter. In modern form it is the induction-head or shift-SSM mechanism, and Memory Mosaics (Zhang et al. 2024b) uses it as a design principle. None of these is wrong. None is novel.

### Anticipated with a twist: GDN state as CA3

Calling the GDN state "CA3" is a relabelling of the fast-weight-as-hippocampus view of Whittington et al. 2022, Tyulmankov et al. 2021 and Gershman et al. 2025. The specific choice of CA3 rather than the CA3→CA1 projection is the questionable part (see the biology section). The only new content is the pairing of tied addressing with DG k-WTA keys, and the repository's own data show that the k-WTA part adds nothing at the tested load.

### Partially novel: recruiting frozen GDN layers through a residual side module

A small (4.38M-parameter) residual module at block 12 of frozen Qwen3.5-2B takes needle recall at a ~501-token gap from 0.00 to 1.00 exact with attention windowed to 64 (P2). Moving the tap to block 22 removes the effect. This review did not find that observation in the literature. The nearest neighbours each differ in an essential way. HLA modifies the GDN layers. State-offset tuning trains offsets inside the SSM. δ-mem adds an external state to a pure-attention model. SWAX trains from scratch. The result is therefore worth pursuing, but the claimed mechanism, that the hippocampal loop routes the needle through downstream GDN layers, is not yet supported. With the default `osc` kernel the loop's weight at Δ ≈ 500 is exactly zero (P3). The loop gain moved only from 0.2689 to 0.2697 during that run. The `TimeChannel` integrates a scalar salience signal, so it cannot carry digit content. The 4.38M parameters therefore act as a trained adapter at block 12: they change what frozen GDN layers 13–21 write at the needle and what they query at the answer. That is closer to state-offset tuning or SWAX-style pressure on the recurrent layers than to hippocampal replay. The obvious control has not been run: a parameter-matched trainable residual MLP with memory and time off.

### Partially novel: the temporal loop as an oscillatory-kernel retrieval

`HippocampalLoop` is an O(T) linear-attention read whose relevance is gated by a fixed lag kernel. Fixed exponential decay (RetNet, in the bibliography) and compressive memories (Infini-attention) cover most of that design space. The clamped damped-sinusoid kernel and the op-amp feedback analysis (T6–T8) are distinctive, but they belong to the loop track and lie outside this review. The P4a finding, a plateau at exactly one digit known and three at chance, is a neat mechanistic diagnosis. The prescribed fix (W1's address shift) is the induction-head shift.

### Not supported by the repository's own data: DG sparsity raises GDN capacity

In `gdn_capacity.json`, `tied_x8` (dense address) and `dg_x8` (5% k-WTA) both reach 1.00 recall at 8, 16 and 32 pairs with the same d_k = 128. Under a linear readout, the theory above predicts exactly this null. Sparsity cannot raise the d_k exact-capacity bound. It can at most reduce crosstalk among non-orthogonal natural keys.

## Biological accuracy issues

The mapping GDN state → CA3 is defensible only at Marr's computational level. At the level of mechanism, five points need correcting or qualifying.

First, CA3 is the classic autoassociative attractor network. Its recurrent collaterals complete degraded patterns by iterated dynamics ([Marr 1971](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1971.0078); [Treves & Rolls 1994](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450040319); [Rolls 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2013.00074)). A GDN read o = S_t q_t is a single linear heteroassociative lookup with no attractor and no iteration. The repository's `ca3.py` (Hopfield softmax) is a closer CA3 analogue than the GDN memory. Tied-mode GDN is better described as a model of CA3's heteroassociative sequence role (Levy 1996; Jensen & Lisman 1996) or of the Schaffer-collateral CA3→CA1 projection, not of CA3 pattern completion.

Second, the learning rule differs. The delta rule is error-corrective: each synapse needs the mismatch v − Sk. CA3 recurrent plasticity is usually modelled as Hebbian or spike-timing-dependent. The best-documented one-shot, instructive plasticity in the hippocampus is behavioural-timescale synaptic plasticity, and it is in CA1, not CA3 ([Bittner et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan3846)). BTSP's seconds-long, asymmetric eligibility window also binds an input to what follows it, a natural biological counterpart of the successor-bound write. A biologically faithful version of GDN's read-then-write is Hasselmo's separation of retrieval and encoding into theta phases ([Hasselmo, Bodelón & Wyble 2002](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976602317318965)). In that scheme the network first predicts at one phase, then encodes the residual at the other. Stated that way, the CA1 "novelty" and the delta-rule error are the same quantity, which the docstring already hints at.

Third, the decay α_t is described as "synaptic decay, LTD". It is a per-head, per-token multiplicative shrinkage of every synapse at once, which is closer to global synaptic down-scaling ([Tononi & Cirelli 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.12.025)) than to input-specific LTD. With the repository's initial bias of −5, α ≈ 0.9933 per token. A stored association therefore keeps exp(500·ln 0.9933) ≈ 3.5% of its strength after 500 tokens unless training pushes α towards 1. Hippocampal episodic traces are not thought to decay on a timescale of a few hundred events; they fail mainly by interference.

Fourth, the DG-to-capacity argument does not carry over. In Treves–Rolls theory, CA3 capacity scales with the number of recurrent synapses per cell and inversely with a·ln(1/a) at sparseness a, and DG mossy fibres matter because they impose sparse, decorrelated codes at encoding ([Treves & Rolls 1992](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450020209)). Those gains rely on a thresholded, non-linear readout. A linear GDN head holds at most d_k clean associations however sparse its keys are, which is what M5/M7 observe. Treves & Rolls also argue that retrieval cues reach CA3 through the perforant path, not through DG. `"dg"` addressing uses the DG code for both write and read, which is the arrangement they argue against. The repository's own T16 tested a version of this and did not support the direct-path advantage.

Fifth, mapping the matrix state to CA3 sits awkwardly with key–value accounts of memory (Gershman et al. 2025), which keep the representations used for retrieval separate from those used for storage. If the hippocampus is read as an index in the sense of indexing theory, an interpretation this review adds rather than one taken from that paper, the GDN key and query projections and the state's addressing structure would be "hippocampal", and the stored values cortical. The repository should either adopt a mapping of that kind or say why it rejects it.

## Proposed experiments

The experiments below are ordered by how much they would change the project's conclusions per GPU-hour. Each names the register items it extends.

**E1: probe the GDN state for the needle (H; extends P1, P2, O5).** The hypothesis is that, in frozen Qwen3.5-2B with attention windowed, the needle digits are linearly recoverable from the GDN state of some layers at the answer position, so P1 is a read failure rather than a write failure. Run the existing needle task with no side channels and record S_t^{(ℓ)} for every GDN layer at the query position. Train two probes per layer and per digit: a ridge probe on the flattened per-head state, and a learned query vector q* whose readout S q* is decoded linearly, which tests whether the state is addressable by some query. Also compute an HLA-style attenuation curve, ‖S_t(with needle) − S_t(needle replaced)‖ / ‖S_t‖, as a function of gap. Controls are a 32-token gap (inside the window), a shuffled-needle control and the Qwen3-1.7B residual stream. The metrics are probe digit accuracy (chance 0.1) by layer and the attenuation slope. No training of side channels is needed, so the experiment costs a few GPU-hours.

**E2: parameter-matched adapter controls for P2 (H; extends P2, P5, O4).** The hypothesis is that any trainable 4.4M-parameter perturbation upstream of the GDN layers reaches the same exact recall, which would make P2 an adaptation result rather than a hippocampal-loop result. All arms use Qwen3.5-2B with attention windowed and gap ~501, and are trained for 3,000 steps with 3 seeds. The arms are:

1. The current side channels.
2. A zero-initialised residual MLP at block 12 with memory and time off, matched in parameters.
3. LoRA on the q/k/v/α/β projections of GDN layers 13–21, matched in parameters.
4. State-offset tuning ([Kang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03499)) on the same layers.
5. The current side channels with the loop's memory read zeroed at evaluation.

The metrics are exact match, token accuracy, per-digit cross-entropy (C1a), and perplexity drift on held-out generic text. If arm 2 matches arm 1, P2 should be retitled.

**E3: state-space injection instead of residual injection (H; extends W1, O3, D-c, S7).** The hypothesis is that writing retrieved content directly into a GDN layer's state, using that layer's own key space, recalls with fewer parameters and less collateral perplexity damage than adding to the residual stream. A side memory (the W1 successor-bound GDN memory, or the horizon-kernel loop) produces (k̂, v̂) at each step. These are injected as S_t ← S_t + γ_t v̂ k̂ᵀ into one or more GDN layers after the tap, with k̂ = norm(W_k^{(ℓ)} x̂) so that the native queries can find it, and γ_t gated by CA1 novelty. The baselines are residual injection at block 12, a learned state offset, and δ-mem-style low-rank attention correction ([Lei et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12357)). The metrics are needle exact match at gaps of 500, 2k and 8k with all layers windowed, WikiText perplexity change, and the injected-state norm ratio, with S1's saturation sweep run first.

**E4: W1 as a head-to-head comparison against δ-mem and same-position keys (H; extends W1, P4a, C1a).** The hypothesis is that the address shift lifts P4a from one digit to four. On Qwen3-1.7B with all layers windowed, attach a fixed-size side GDN memory (d_k ∈ {64, 256}) with three addressing arms: same-position keys (the current loop), tied successor keys a_{t−1} ↦ v_t, and a learned short-conv shift as in standard GDN. Add a δ-mem reproduction. The metrics are per-digit cross-entropy against the k-digits-known ladder (1.842 / 1.382 / 0.921 / …), exact match, and a pairs sweep up to 2·d_k.

**E5: a capacity sweep that can falsify "exact RAM" (M; corrects M4, M5, M7).** Theory predicts that a linear readout stays exact until P ≈ d_k for orthogonal keys and then degrades smoothly, that DG sparsity does not move this boundary, and that a softmax or thresholded readout does. Train pure-GDN small LMs on the existing `AssociativeRecall` task with P ∈ {8, …, 512}, n_keys ∈ {64, 1024} and d_k ∈ {16, 64, 128}. The arms are `gdn` (with the conv initialised to a previous-token tap), `tied`, `dg`, DeltaProduct with two Householder steps, a modern-Hopfield readout, and a properly tuned softmax-attention baseline. Each arm gets a learning-rate sweep ([Okpekpe & Orvieto 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19029)) and 3 seeds. The metric is recall against P/d_k. This also rescues M4 by separating optimisation from capacity.

**E6: a fair Titans comparison (M; extends M6, J3).** Use a per-token Titans update (chunk = 1, no averaged η or α, no boundary drop), or the authors' reference implementation, with matched state size and 3 seeds. Re-tune the full-attention baseline until it solves the 6-pair task, as a sanity check on the task. The metric is recall and its learning curve.

**E7: causal tracing of the needle route in the hybrid (M; extends O5, P1).** Run activation and state patching between clean and corrupted needles, following Meng et al. 2022 (in the bibliography), with full attention on. This finds which GDN layers and which of the six attention layers carry the needle. Then identify Gather-and-Aggregate heads ([Bick, Xing & Gu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18574)) and test whether windowing only those heads reproduces P1. The metric is the patching recovery fraction by layer and head.

**E8: consolidating GDN state into weights for never-ending chat (M; extends W3, M3, O1).** The hypothesis is that, at segment boundaries, distilling what the GDN state and window hold into a LoRA ("sleep") preserves recall of evicted facts better than keeping it in the state. Feed Qwen3.5-2B a stream of segments that contain facts queried K segments later. The arms are: state only; sleep into LoRA with uniform replay; sleep with novelty priority; sleep with priority by the GDN self-recall error; offline recurrence into the state ([Lee et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099)); and SDFT ([Shenfeld et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897)). The metrics are fact recall as a function of K, backward transfer, and general capability (perplexity and an MMLU subset).

**E9: a three-tier memory for the KV-trimming project (L; extends O3).** Put a LoLA-style split on Qwen3.5: windowed KV in the attention layers, a sparse exact cache admitted by GDN self-recall error (the `err` already computed in `gated_delta_scan`), and the GDN state for everything else. The baselines are SnapKV ([Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14469)), H2O ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048)) and StreamingLLM ([Xiao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)). The metrics are RULER needle accuracy at 32k–128k ([Hsieh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654)) against cache bytes.

**E10: multi-hop successor retrieval as pathfinding (L; extends W1, D-b).** The hypothesis, following Samsonovich & Ascoli's pathfinding view of CA3, is that iterated reads of a tied-address GDN memory (read v_{t+1}, use it as the next query) solve multi-hop chain retrieval that a single read cannot. As a cheap side arm, allow β ∈ (0, 2) ([Grazzi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537)) to test state tracking. The metric is accuracy against hop count on a synthetic graph-walk task.

## Open questions

The first unresolved question is whether Qwen3.5's GDN layers fail on the 500-token needle at write time or at read time. HLA's attenuation result points to write and retention, while the G&A literature points to read. E1 decides between them cheaply, and every later design choice depends on the answer. The second is whether anything is gained by calling a GDN head "CA3". If the computational claims are the published fast-weight identities, the biological label earns its keep only if it predicts something the ML framing does not. Candidates include theta-phase separation of read and write, BTSP-like successor binding with a seconds-scale eligibility trace, or interference-limited rather than decay-limited forgetting. None has yet been tested. The third is whether DG sparsity has any role under a linear readout. Theory says it cannot raise exact capacity, but it might reduce crosstalk among correlated natural-language keys, and E5 with natural-text keys would show this. The fourth is how successor-bound addressing should generalise beyond the previous token. In text the useful key for a later answer is a multi-token cue, which a short convolution or attention-built context supplies and a single tied address does not. The fifth concerns the never-ending-chat goal: how much should be consolidated into weights, held in a fixed state, or kept as exact KV? The 2026 sleep papers, TTT-E2E and LoLA each answer differently, and no study compares them on one model under one memory budget. E8 and E9 together would be that comparison.
